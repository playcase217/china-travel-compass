Xi'an deserves two city days in addition to the Terracotta Warriors. The center layers a Ming city wall over the much larger footprint of imperial Chang'an, while museums, pagodas, mosques, markets, and neighborhood food reveal histories that one famous excavation cannot.

Use day one for the wall and old center, then day two for a museum and the Giant Wild Goose Pagoda area. Put the Terracotta Warriors on a separate day so traffic, security, and museum time do not turn the city into a rushed evening stop.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** imperial history, archaeology, architecture, calligraphy, and regional food.
- **Time needed:** two city days plus one separate Warriors day.
- **Simple plan:** wall and old center on day one; museum and pagoda district on day two.
- **Main planning issue:** major museums and attractions can require advance reservations or have weekly closing days.
- **Editorial view:** Xi'an makes more sense when you connect objects in museums with the streets and monuments outside.

## Day one: understand the walled center

Begin around Yongning Gate and ascend the Xi'an City Wall if weather and access permit. A full circuit is longer than it appears; walking one well-chosen section can be more rewarding than treating completion as a test. Heat, wind, rain, maintenance, and event closures can affect entrances and bicycle services, so check the wall's current notice before setting out.

Continue toward the Forest of Stone Steles Museum if calligraphy and carved records interest you. Renovation can change galleries and entrances. Otherwise, use Shuyuanmen and the lanes inside the wall as a slower transition toward the Bell and Drum Tower area.

Visit the Muslim Quarter as a lived commercial and religious neighborhood, not just a single snack street. Beiyuanmen is busy and theatrical; quieter surrounding lanes can offer a broader view. Dress and behave respectfully around mosques, ask before photographing people, and do not assume every stall represents the whole cuisine of Xi'an's Hui communities.

## Day two: choose one museum, then go south

Choose the Shaanxi History Museum, Xi'an Museum, or another major collection based on current exhibitions and reservation availability. Do not attempt several large museums in one morning. Passport rules, release times, closed days, and entry channels can change, and unofficial ticket bundles are not a substitute for the venue's own instructions.

In the afternoon, continue to the Giant Wild Goose Pagoda and Da Ci'en Temple area. The pagoda is part of the UNESCO-listed Silk Roads property and is associated with the transmission and translation of Buddhist texts. Separate the historic temple visit from the surrounding modern plazas and entertainment district; both can be enjoyable, but they tell different stories.

Evening light and performances draw crowds south of the pagoda. Check temporary controls and the last practical route back rather than relying on an old show schedule.

## Keep the Terracotta Warriors separate

The Emperor Qinshihuang's Mausoleum Site Museum is outside central Xi'an and merits several hours. Reserve through the current official channel, use the passport entered in the booking, and allow for road or transit delays. Follow our [Terracotta Warriors guide](/guides/xian-terracotta-warriors-guide/) for the detailed plan.

## Arrival and practical planning

Xi'an has multiple rail stations. Search the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/) for your date and match the exact station to your hotel. Metro and airport arrangements can change, so verify the current official map after booking.

- Save Xi'an as 西安 and Yongning Gate as 永宁门.
- Carry the passport used for reservations.
- Check museum closed days before assigning itinerary days.
- Protect against summer heat on the exposed wall.
- Keep a backup payment method and some cash.
- Allow more time during national holidays and major events.
