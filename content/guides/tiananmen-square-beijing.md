Tiananmen Square is worth visiting for its scale, central location, and place in Beijing's modern civic landscape. The visit is straightforward when you reserve in advance, carry your passport, expect security screening, and pair the square with a timed Forbidden City entry.

## At a glance

Admission to Tiananmen Square is free, but reservations are required. International visitors should use an accepted reservation route and bring the same identity document used for booking. Leave extra time for security checks and confirm current procedures shortly before your visit.

## Why include the square

The square is best understood as part of a wider central Beijing walk. Its open scale contrasts sharply with the courtyards and gates immediately to the north.

For many first-time visitors, the strongest plan is not to treat it as a standalone day. Combine it with the Forbidden City and move through the historic center in a logical sequence.

## Plan the visit around your museum ticket

Reserve the square and the Forbidden City separately. If your palace entry is in the morning, arrive early enough to complete security checks without stress.

Carry your original passport and keep reservation details accessible on your phone. Procedures can change, so verify the latest instructions before departure rather than relying on an old screenshot or travel forum post.

## What to expect

The square is large and exposed. In summer, sun protection matters. In winter, wind can make the open space feel colder than nearby streets.

Expect controlled entry points and identity checks. Keep bags simple and allow enough time to find the correct route.

## A smart central Beijing itinerary

Start at Tiananmen Square, continue north into the [Forbidden City](/guides/forbidden-city-beijing/), and finish with the view from Jingshan Park if you still have energy. This route turns separate landmarks into a coherent first introduction to Beijing.
