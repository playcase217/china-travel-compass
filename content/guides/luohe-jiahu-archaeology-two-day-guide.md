Luohe offers an unusual pairing: internationally important Neolithic archaeology in Wuyang County and a modern city strongly associated with food processing and trade. Two days work when you treat Jiahu as a research-led heritage visit, not as a promise that every famous object or excavation trench will be visible.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** archaeology, early music history, river-city walks, and modern urban economy.
- **Time needed:** two days.
- **Simple plan:** Luohe museum and river context; then a pre-arranged Wuyang and Jiahu visit if officially open.
- **Main planning issue:** the Jiahu site is outside central Luohe, while important finds are held by museums and may not be displayed locally.
- **Editorial view:** understanding provenance matters more than chasing a photograph of one celebrated artifact.

## Day one: establish the archaeological story

Start with a confirmed-open municipal museum or cultural venue in Luohe. Read the local chronology before planning the county trip: Jiahu was a Neolithic settlement in present-day Wuyang County, and finds from the site have shaped research into early music, subsistence, craft, and ritual life.

The best-known objects are bone flutes. The Henan Museum identifies its Jiahu bone flute as coming from the Wuyang site and dates the settlement to roughly 7,800–9,000 years ago. Do not imply that this specific flute is on display in Luohe; verify the location of any object and current exhibition directly with the holding museum.

Use the afternoon for a public walk along an officially open urban riverfront. River confluences and flood-control landscapes help explain Luohe's geography, but embankments, weirs, working quays, and water-control structures are not informal viewpoints.

## Day two: Wuyang and Jiahu, only with confirmed access

Arrange a return vehicle and verify whether the 贾湖遗址 visitor facility, exhibition, or designated viewing area is operating on your date. An archaeological site can close for research, conservation, construction, weather, or staffing. Never enter fields, active trenches, storage areas, or fenced land in search of the site.

If public access cannot be confirmed, substitute a museum day in Luohe rather than improvising. A responsible fallback could include public city-history displays and an exterior look at the modern food-industry landscape. Factory tours are only appropriate when an operator explicitly offers a public visit; production areas, loading yards, laboratories, and worker entrances are controlled workplaces.

## Transport, food, and weather

Verify trains and the exact Luohe station through the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/). Save 漯河站, 漯河西站, 舞阳县, and 贾湖遗址 in Chinese. Confirm the Wuyang return before leaving the city and do not assume a ride-hailing car will accept a rural pickup.

Luohe's food identity is interesting, but a brand name or packaged product does not prove that a factory accepts visitors. For eating, choose busy licensed premises, communicate allergies clearly, and keep packaging when checking ingredients. Use the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) for heat, storms, flooding, fog, snow, and ice.
