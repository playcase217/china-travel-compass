Zunyi works best as a two-part history trip: one day for the modern city and its museums, then one day for Hailongtun, the mountainous fortress component of UNESCO's serial Tusi Sites property. The two stories belong to very different periods, so resist compressing them into a rushed checklist.

Stay two nights in central Zunyi and give the archaeological day a weather buffer.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** Chinese history, archaeology, museums, mountain landscapes, and Guizhou food.
- **Time needed:** two full days plus arrival and departure buffers.
- **Simple plan:** central museums and streets; Hailongtun as a dedicated excursion.
- **Main planning issue:** advance-entry rules, mountain walking, rain, and the last return transfer.
- **Editorial view:** use museums and interpretation to connect evidence with history instead of treating each site as a photo backdrop.

## Day one: build the city context

Choose one or two currently open official museums or memorial institutions in central Zunyi. Check their own notices for passport acceptance, reservations, security screening, photography, closed days, and final admission. A foreign passport may require a staffed counter even when domestic visitors use an ID-based mini program.

Leave time for an ordinary neighborhood walk and a Guizhou meal. Ask about chili level rather than assuming a dish is mild, and confirm ingredients if you have allergies. Do not photograph ceremonies, school groups, security areas, or identifiable visitors without permission.

## Day two: Hailongtun

UNESCO lists Hailongtun with Laosicheng and Tangya as the three components of the Tusi Sites. Hailongtun preserves evidence of a mountain administrative and defensive center; it is not simply a detached castle viewpoint.

Confirm the official entrance, current ticket process, internal transport, walking route, final admission, and weather closures before leaving the city. Wear shoes with grip and carry water and rain protection. Wet stone, steep steps, heat, fog, and limited shade can make the outing more demanding than the distance suggests.

Follow the marked visitor route, do not climb walls or enter closed archaeological areas, and avoid touching masonry. UNESCO notes vulnerability to vegetation and heavy-rain erosion, which makes on-site closures part of conservation rather than an inconvenience to bypass.

## Transport and onward planning

- Save Zunyi as 遵义 and Hailongtun as 海龙屯.
- Ask your hotel to write the full official entrance name for a driver; a bare attraction name is not enough.
- Check trains only on the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/).
- Pair Zunyi with [Guiyang](/guides/guiyang-two-day-itinerary/) as a separate stop.
