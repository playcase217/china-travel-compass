Zhenjiang is a compact Jiangsu stop where hills, temples, a former Yangtze crossing, and canal-era trade history sit close to a major rail corridor. It is less polished for international tourism than Nanjing or Suzhou, which is part of its appeal—but it rewards travelers who save Chinese place names and verify details locally.

Spend two days. Walk Xijindu and visit the museum area on day one, then choose two of Zhenjiang’s famous hill landscapes on day two. Jinshan and Beigushan make the most coherent first visit; Jiaoshan is a useful alternative rather than a compulsory third stop.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** temple landscapes, river history, old streets, literature, and a quieter Jiangsu city.
- **Time needed:** two days; a focused day trip works from Nanjing.
- **Simple plan:** Xijindu and museum context on day one; Jinshan and Beigushan on day two.
- **Main planning issue:** “Three Hills” sounds compact, but each deserves time and access can change.
- **Best approach:** use taxis or local transit selectively and keep the route geographically sensible.

## Day one: Xijindu and the old crossing

Xijindu developed around an important historic crossing near the Yangtze and the Grand Canal network. Today its sloping lanes contain religious buildings, former institutional sites, restored structures, shops, and museums. The district has evolved over many periods; it is not a frozen ancient street.

Walk uphill slowly and look for changes in paving, street level, building use, and orientation toward the former waterfront. Commercial sections can become busy, but side lanes and museum displays provide context. Respect closed doors and do not treat every courtyard as public.

Combine the district with Zhenjiang Museum if its current opening day and reservation rules work. A museum visit makes the next day’s religious and river landscapes easier to interpret.

## Day two morning: Jinshan

Jinshan is closely associated with Jinshan Temple and with stories that have accumulated through literature, religion, and popular culture, including versions of the Legend of the White Snake. Visit for the layered hill-and-temple composition, not to verify a folktale as history.

Stairs and crowds shape the experience. Start early, follow temple rules, and keep voices low where worship is active. Photography restrictions may differ by hall. Rain or ice can make steep paving hazardous.

## Day two afternoon: Beigushan

Beigushan offers a different relationship between hill, buildings, history, and Yangtze views. Ganlu Temple and the hill’s literary associations connect the site to stories of the Three Kingdoms, but interpretation again mixes documented history with later tradition.

Choose a shorter route if energy is low. The value lies in the sequence of paths and viewpoints, not simply reaching every marker. Confirm the final exit and transport pickup point before dusk.

Jiaoshan, an island-hill landscape, can replace Beigushan for travelers more interested in inscriptions, quieter scenery, or a boat-linked visit. Its access arrangement is more operationally sensitive, so verify before departure.

## Vinegar, food, and souvenirs

Zhenjiang is famous for aromatic dark vinegar. Taste it as part of local cooking before buying a large bottle, and check airline liquid and baggage rules for onward travel. “Zhenjiang vinegar” covers different producers and styles; avoid claims that one shop is the only authentic source.

At restaurants, ask portion size and whether a dish contains meat stock, shellfish, peanuts, or other allergens. Translation apps help, but they do not replace confirmation for serious allergies.

## Getting there

Zhenjiang lies on busy Jiangsu rail routes, but the city has multiple station names. Search the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/) for your actual date, confirm the station, and carry the booking passport.

Zhenjiang works well between [Nanjing](/guides/nanjing-two-day-itinerary/) and [Suzhou](/guides/suzhou-day-trip-from-shanghai/), but do not assume every train stops at the same station. Leave a buffer after hill visits, especially in rain or holiday traffic.

## Where to stay

A location between Xijindu, the central rail connection, and the city core reduces transfers. Confirm directly that the hotel can honor the booking and complete foreign-guest accommodation registration. Save its entrance—not only the building complex—in Chinese.

## Accessibility and respectful travel

Hills, stairs, old paving, and temple thresholds make full step-free access unlikely. Ask each scenic area about current accessible routes and shuttle services. Do not fly a drone, touch inscriptions, burn incense outside designated areas, or photograph worshippers without permission.

Useful Chinese names:

- Xijindu: 西津渡
- Jinshan: 金山
- Beigushan: 北固山
- Jiaoshan: 焦山
- Zhenjiang Museum: 镇江博物馆
