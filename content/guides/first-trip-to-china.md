China rewards a little preparation. The transport network is extensive, cities are generally easy to navigate, and everyday travel can be remarkably smooth once your phone, payment methods, and key bookings are ready.

This guide covers the setup that matters most before your first visit. Entry rules can vary by nationality and change over time, so confirm the latest requirements with the Chinese embassy or consulate that serves your place of residence before booking.

## Before you book

Start with three checks:

- Confirm whether you need a visa, qualify for a visa-free entry policy, or can use a transit policy for your route.
- Make sure your passport validity comfortably covers the trip and any airline requirements.
- Choose an itinerary with enough time in each place. China is large, and rushing through too many cities creates unnecessary travel days.

For a first visit, Beijing, Shanghai, and one additional destination make a balanced introduction. Xi'an works well for history; Chengdu suits food and a slower pace; Hangzhou is convenient if you want scenery close to Shanghai.

## Set up your phone before arrival

Mobile data makes independent travel much easier. You will use your phone for maps, translations, train bookings, ride-hailing, and payments.

An international roaming package or travel eSIM is usually the simplest option to arrange before departure. A local SIM may offer better value for a longer stay, but registration and compatibility can add friction after arrival. Check whether your phone supports eSIM and whether your chosen plan works in mainland China.

Download an offline translation pack and save the Chinese name and address of your first hotel. A screenshot is useful if your connection is not ready when you land.

## Prepare more than one way to pay

Mobile payment is widespread in China. Visitors can often link eligible international cards to major payment apps, but you should set this up before your flight and keep a backup.

Bring:

- A payment app configured with an eligible card.
- A second physical bank card.
- A modest amount of cash for backup.
- Your bank's support contact details.

Rules, limits, and supported cards can change. Check the official instructions inside your selected payment app shortly before departure. Never depend on only one payment method.

## Use high-speed rail for city-to-city travel

China's high-speed rail network is often the best option between major cities. It connects city centers, avoids airport transfer time, and is comfortable for medium-distance journeys.

![A traveler boarding a high-speed train at a modern station](/images/guides/first-trip-to-china/high-speed-train.webp)

Book using the official railway booking channel or a reputable booking platform. Your passport details must match the booking exactly. Arrive early enough for security checks and station navigation, especially at large stations with multiple entrances.

## Choose hotels that can register foreign guests

Hotels are required to handle guest registration. When booking, check that the property accepts international travelers and use the same passport name shown on your travel document.

Save the hotel address in both English and Chinese. If you take a taxi, the Chinese address is usually more useful than the English hotel name.

## A comfortable 7-day first itinerary

### Days 1-3: Beijing

Use Beijing for China's imperial history and large-scale landmarks. Allow time for the Forbidden City area, a Great Wall day trip, and one neighborhood walk. Reserve major attractions in advance where required.

### Days 4-5: Xi'an or Hangzhou

Choose Xi'an for the Terracotta Warriors and a strong historical contrast with Beijing. Choose Hangzhou for a gentler pace, lakeside scenery, and an easier onward connection to Shanghai.

### Days 6-7: Shanghai

Finish in Shanghai for waterfront views, varied dining, and a convenient international departure point. Mix the Bund and central neighborhoods with one quieter area rather than trying to cover the entire city.

## Small habits that make the trip easier

- Carry your passport when required for transport or attraction entry.
- Keep hotel addresses and essential bookings available offline.
- Allow extra time at major railway stations.
- Use official sources for entry policies and bookings.
- Learn a few simple phrases, but rely on translation tools when precision matters.

China is easier to explore when the essentials are ready before arrival. Once those are in place, leave some room in the itinerary for local neighborhoods, regional food, and slower days between the headline sights.
