English-speaking visitors can pay for most everyday purchases in China with Alipay or WeChat Pay, but the safest setup is not one app. Prepare two mobile payment options where possible, keep a physical bank card, carry a small amount of cash, and test your payment setup before you need it at a busy restaurant, taxi pickup, or train station.

## At a glance

Set up Alipay and WeChat Pay before your trip and link eligible international cards. Test a small payment soon after arrival. Keep cash and a physical card as backups. For QR payments, either scan the merchant's code or show your payment code when asked. If one app fails, switch to the other app, another card, or cash rather than trying to fix everything at the counter.

## Why payment planning matters in China

Mobile payment is deeply embedded in daily life in China. Restaurants, convenience stores, cafes, taxis, attractions, vending machines, and local services often expect QR payments.

Foreign visitors are not excluded from this system, but setup details can vary by app, card issuer, identity verification, network access, and merchant type. A little preparation removes most friction.

## The best default setup

Before departure, prepare:

- Alipay with an eligible international card.
- WeChat Pay with an eligible international card where supported.
- A physical Visa, Mastercard, or other international bank card.
- A modest amount of RMB cash.
- Working mobile data.

This combination gives you flexibility. If your card issuer blocks one transaction, or a merchant's QR code works better with one app, you have another route.

## Alipay: a strong first payment choice

Alipay is often the easiest first mobile wallet for visitors because it provides international-facing guidance and supports many travel-related services.

Set it up before you arrive:

- Install the app from an official app store.
- Register your account.
- Complete identity steps if requested.
- Link an eligible international bank card.
- Test the app interface before travel day.

After arrival, make a small purchase to confirm your card and network work correctly.

## WeChat Pay: useful as a second wallet

WeChat is widely used in China for communication, mini programs, and payments. Even if Alipay is your main wallet, WeChat Pay is a useful backup.

It can help when:

- A merchant prefers WeChat Pay.
- A local contact sends a WeChat-based payment request.
- A mini program works inside WeChat.
- You need a second route after a failed transaction.

Some features are more complicated for visitors than for long-term residents, so treat WeChat Pay as a practical second wallet rather than your only payment plan.

## How QR payments usually work

There are two common payment flows:

- **You scan the merchant's QR code:** enter the amount if required, confirm, and show the success screen.
- **The merchant scans your payment code:** open the payment code in your app and let the cashier scan it.

Check the amount before confirming. If you are unsure, use translation or ask the cashier to show the number on a calculator or payment screen.

## Cash still matters

China is highly mobile-payment friendly, but cash remains a useful backup. Carry enough RMB for small emergencies, transport problems, or payment-app failures.

Do not carry too much cash. A modest backup is enough for a meal, taxi, or short-term problem. Keep small notes if possible.

## International cards and ATMs

International cards may work at some hotels, larger stores, and ATMs, but they are not a universal everyday payment method in smaller businesses.

Before you travel:

- Tell your bank you will be in China if your bank recommends travel notices.
- Check foreign transaction fees.
- Confirm ATM withdrawal limits.
- Bring more than one card if possible.
- Save your bank's support number.

If a card-linked mobile wallet transaction fails, the problem may be the bank, card network, merchant type, app verification, spending limit, or network connection.

## Mobile data is part of payment

Payment apps need reliable internet access. If your phone has no data, your payment setup may be useless at the exact moment you need it.

Arrange roaming, eSIM, or a local SIM before depending on QR payments. The [China internet and SIM guide](/guides/china-internet-sim-esim-guide/) explains how to choose a connectivity setup.

## Common payment problems and what to do

If payment fails, stay practical:

- Try the other payment app.
- Try a different linked card.
- Check whether your mobile data is working.
- Ask whether the merchant can accept cash.
- Use a physical card if the merchant supports it.
- Call your bank later if the card appears blocked.

Do not keep retrying the same failed payment many times in a row. That can trigger fraud controls or duplicate confusion.

## Where payment setup matters most

Make sure your payment works before:

- Taking a taxi or ride-hailing trip.
- Eating at a small restaurant.
- Buying metro or transport services.
- Booking attractions.
- Buying train tickets.
- Paying rent, deposits, or long-stay expenses.

For transport, see the [China high-speed rail guide](/guides/china-high-speed-rail-guide/) and [Beijing subway guide](/guides/beijing-subway-guide/). For everyday app setup, use the [China apps guide](/guides/china-apps-for-english-speakers/).

## A simple pre-flight checklist

- Install Alipay and WeChat from official app stores.
- Link eligible international cards.
- Bring at least two physical cards if possible.
- Carry a modest amount of RMB cash.
- Arrange mobile data.
- Save your bank's support contact.
- Test payment with a small purchase after arrival.

Payment in China is manageable for English-speaking visitors, but it rewards redundancy. Set up the apps, keep backups, and test early. Once your payment system works, daily travel becomes much smoother.
