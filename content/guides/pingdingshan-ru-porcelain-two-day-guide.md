The Pingdingshan region is one of China's most rewarding places to study ceramics, but its key sites are spread across separate county-level areas. A practical two-day visit chooses one Ru-porcelain cluster—Ruzhou or the Qingliangsi area—then uses the second day for museum context or another pre-arranged ceramic stop.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** ceramics, kiln archaeology, craft processes, museum travelers, and collectors who value provenance.
- **Time needed:** two days; more if combining distant counties.
- **Simple plan:** a Ru-porcelain museum or authorized workshop; then Qingliangsi or one other verified heritage site.
- **Main planning issue:** Pingdingshan city, Ruzhou, Baofeng, and Lushan are not one walkable destination.
- **Editorial view:** learn how clay, glaze, firing, archaeology, and modern revival differ before shopping.

## Day one: museum first, workshop second

Begin at a confirmed-open Ru-porcelain museum or public cultural venue. Pingdingshan's culture and tourism bureau identifies the China Ru Porcelain Town in Ruzhou as a cluster combining a specialist museum, modern makers, and craft interpretation. Verify the exact venue, closed day, passport handling, and whether demonstrations require advance contact.

Look for clear labels distinguishing excavated shards, reconstructed kiln evidence, later collections, and contemporary work. “Ru ware,” “Ru-style,” and “made in Ruzhou” are not automatic guarantees of date, technique, or authenticity. At a workshop, ask before photographing people, tools, recipes, or unfinished pieces.

When buying, request an itemized receipt and written material description. Treat unverifiable imperial-kiln claims, artificial scarcity, and pressure to ship immediately as reasons to pause.

## Day two: Qingliangsi archaeology or a slower ceramic day

If current public access and transport are confirmed, devote the day to the 清凉寺汝官窑遗址 area in Baofeng County. Municipal planning materials identify Qingliangsi as a core Ru-porcelain archaeological resource. Archaeological parks and exhibitions can close for conservation, research, or construction; use only the official entrance and never collect shards, clay, kiln furniture, or material from surrounding land.

If access is uncertain, remain with the Ruzhou museum-and-maker cluster instead. This is more useful than attempting to add Yaoshan, distant county museums, and multiple workshops in one day. Mountain tourism around Pingdingshan deserves a separate itinerary with its own weather and trail planning.

## Transport and responsible collecting

Search Pingdingshan, Ruzhou, and the exact station on the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/). Save 平顶山, 汝州, 宝丰, 中国汝瓷博物馆, and 清凉寺汝官窑遗址 in Chinese. Confirm both legs of any county transfer before departure.

Do not carry soil-covered objects, antiquities, or undocumented “old shards.” China's cultural-property rules are serious, and archaeological material should remain in its legal context. For weather affecting road transfers, use the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/).
