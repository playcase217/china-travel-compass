Lishui is often used as a label for a large sweep of southwestern Zhejiang, but the city center and its outlying counties are not one compact attraction. Two days work well when the first is devoted to local history and the second to Guyan Huaxiang, where an ancient irrigation landscape and a modern painting community occupy different sides of the river story.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** regional museums, river landscapes, photography, painting culture, and slower Zhejiang routes.
- **Time needed:** two days.
- **Simple plan:** Lishui Museum and the historic city; Guyan Huaxiang on the better-weather day.
- **Main planning issue:** the “Guyan” and “Huaxiang” components may involve separate access, road approaches, or a boat connection.
- **Editorial view:** understand the working water landscape before treating the villages as picture backdrops.

## Day one: begin with Chuzhou and the Ou River

Start at 丽水市博物馆, which official city reporting identifies as a national first-grade museum. Check the museum's current public notice for hours, passport registration, temporary exhibitions, and evening programming. Its collections help place the old Chuzhou prefecture, mountain communities, waterways, crafts, and the modern city in the same regional frame.

Continue through a confirmed-open historic street such as 刘祠堂背 or use a public riverside route. The city government describes Liu Citangbei as surviving Ming–Qing urban fabric renewed without wholesale demolition. Remember that an old street remains a lived and commercial place: keep doorways clear and ask before photographing residents.

## Day two: separate the ancient weir from the painting village

Guyan Huaxiang is in Liandu District, outside the central urban itinerary. Official planning documents connect the destination with Tongjiyan's water history and with exhibition, painting, and creative spaces around the art village. Confirm the current visitor-center location, which components are open, and whether the route you want depends on a boat.

Water levels, fog, rain, maintenance, and flood controls can alter crossings and waterfront access. Never step onto a closed embankment or irrigation structure for a photograph. In galleries and studios, ask about photography and distinguish locally made work from generic souvenirs.

If boats are suspended, use the accessible side well rather than improvising a private crossing or forcing both halves into the day. Keep Jinyun, Yunhe, Longquan, and Jingning for separate itineraries.

## Arrival and practical planning

Check rail services on the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/) and save 丽水站, 丽水市博物馆, 古堰画乡, 大港头镇, and 碧湖镇 in Chinese. Confirm the final road transfer and return transport before leaving the city.

The Ou River basin can experience intense rain, heat, typhoons, flooding, fog, and landslide risk. Consult the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) and local closure notices.
