Gongyi's Northern Song imperial tombs are a dispersed archaeological landscape rather than a single enclosed attraction. Surviving spirit-road sculptures stand near modern villages and fields, while public interpretation and access differ from one location to another.

The best first visit begins with a confirmed-open museum or staffed heritage point, then continues to one clearly identified tomb area with local transport arranged both ways.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** Song history, stone sculpture, archaeology, and travelers based in Zhengzhou or Luoyang.
- **Time needed:** one full day with advance planning.
- **Simple plan:** Gongyi museum context; one public tomb landscape.
- **Main planning issue:** scattered sites, ambiguous map pins, limited English interpretation, and return transport.
- **Editorial view:** this is a protected cultural landscape shared with living communities, not an abandoned sculpture park.

## Start with context

Check whether Gongyi Museum or another official interpretation venue is open, whether a passport reservation is needed, and which galleries are accessible. Learn the basic layout of a Song imperial mausoleum before approaching individual stone figures; it makes the surviving alignments much easier to understand.

Ask staff to write the precise Chinese name of a suitable public tomb area and confirm that visitors are currently permitted. The municipal heritage authority describes the imperial tombs as a large group whose stone carvings remain in their original landscape. That does not mean every mound, field, or excavation area is open.

## Visit one tomb landscape

Use a licensed taxi or arranged car and agree on the return plan before leaving central Gongyi. At the site, remain on public roads and signed paths. Do not enter crops, climb a burial mound, touch or lean against sculpture, make rubbings, or move offerings.

Observe the sequence and paired arrangement of the stone figures from a respectful distance. Photograph without blocking residents, agricultural vehicles, or narrow village roads. If conservation staff close an area, choose another official site rather than searching for an unguarded approach.

## Getting there and continuing

- Save Gongyi as 巩义 and the Song imperial tombs as 北宋皇陵 or 宋陵.
- Use the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/) and check the exact Gongyi station on your ticket.
- Keep a large buffer for the return train because local road transport may be slow to summon.
- Combine on separate days with [Luoyang](/guides/luoyang-two-day-city-itinerary/) or [Kaifeng](/guides/kaifeng-two-day-itinerary/) for a wider Song-history route.
