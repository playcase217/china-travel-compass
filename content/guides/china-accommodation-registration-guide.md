Foreign visitors must have their accommodation registered in China. A hotel normally completes this after checking your passport; for a private home or other non-hotel stay, you or the host must complete the local process. The safest approach is to confirm the arrangement before arrival and keep the registration record accessible.

## At a glance

- **Hotel:** present the original passport used for the booking and let the hotel register the stay.
- **Private home or non-hotel stay:** the foreign guest or host must register with the local public security authority within 24 hours of arrival.
- **Online registration:** a national immigration service began as a seven-region pilot in March 2026; do not assume it is available everywhere.
- **Best habit:** ask the property exactly how it will handle foreign-guest registration before a non-refundable booking.

## What hotels do at check-in

China's National Immigration Administration states that hotels register foreign guests and submit the accommodation information to local public security authorities. Bring your original valid passport and use the same name and document number as the booking.

A booking confirmation is not proof that every detail is ready. Message the property directly, especially for a small inn, apartment-style listing, rural guesthouse, or late arrival. Ask whether reception will be staffed, whether it can process your passport, and whether any local access or check-in step applies.

Avoid the misleading phrase “hotels that accept foreigners” as if only a special national category may host overseas guests. The practical question is whether this specific licensed property can honor your reservation and complete the required registration correctly on your date.

## Staying with friends, relatives, or in a private home

For accommodation other than a hotel, the NIA says the foreign visitor or the person providing the accommodation must complete registration within 24 hours after arrival. The responsible local office, supporting documents, and digital options can vary, so contact the local police station or exit-entry service before the deadline if the route is unclear.

Prepare the guest's passport, the full Chinese address, the host's identity and contact details, and evidence connecting the host to the residence if requested. Do not copy a checklist from another city and assume it is complete locally.

## The 2026 online pilot

The NIA launched an online service on March 20, 2026 for foreigners staying in non-hotel residences in Hebei, Liaoning, Zhejiang, Hubei, Guangxi, Chongqing, and Sichuan. Its official channels include the NIA Government Service Platform and NIA 12367 app or mini-program services.

The pilot scope may expand. Check the current NIA guidance rather than assuming an old province list remains complete. If the online route is unavailable or fails, use the responsible local public security office; the NIA identifies 12367 as an inquiry channel.

## Moving, returning, and keeping records

A new city or residence can create a new registration obligation. Long-term residents may have limited simplifications when returning to a registered habitual or self-owned residence, but short-term travelers should not infer an exemption from those rules.

Save a screenshot or copy of the completed record when one is provided. It can be useful if an immigration, visa, or residence-permit process later asks for proof. Registration does not replace a valid visa, stay permit, residence permit, or any separate building and community requirement.

## A practical check-in checklist

- Book with the passport name you will present.
- Save the property's Chinese name, address, phone number, and entrance.
- Confirm reception hours before a late arrival.
- Ask how a private or apartment stay will be registered.
- Keep the original passport secure but available at check-in.
- Recheck official instructions whenever you change accommodation type or address.
