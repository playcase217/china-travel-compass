Chizhou is commonly treated only as a transport base for Jiuhuashan. The city itself offers a quieter Anhui itinerary built around the literary identity of Xinghua Village, surviving historic traces, and the broad water-and-hill landscape of Qishan–Pingtian Lake.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** poetry, low-key city walks, lake landscapes, and travelers who prefer a gentle pace.
- **Time needed:** two days in the city; keep Jiuhuashan as a separate trip.
- **Simple plan:** Xinghua Village and local history; Qishan–Pingtian Lake and the riverfront.
- **Main planning issue:** similarly named attractions and modern interpretation can blur what is historic.
- **Editorial view:** read the poetry connection critically and enjoy the modern landscape without pretending every scene is ancient.

## Day one: Xinghua Village and city memory

Start at 杏花村文化旅游区 (Xinghua Village Cultural Tourism Area). The Chizhou government connects the place with Du Mu's celebrated poem and explains that today's visitor landscape includes reconstructed and newly designed cultural features. Appreciate the literary tradition while separating documented historic elements from contemporary scenic interpretation.

Continue to a currently open local museum or historic area. Chizhou's 2025 protected-area list identifies Xinghua Village, Baiya Hefeng, and the Lower Qingxi Sluice–Qingxi Pagoda surroundings as historic districts. Protection status does not guarantee that every building is open, restored, or designed for visitors, so follow signs and avoid residential or work areas.

## Day two: Qishan and Pingtian Lake

Use the second day for 齐山—平天湖 (Qishan–Pingtian Lake). Choose a public lakeside walk, marked hill path, or operating bicycle route based on weather and current access. The city promotes the area as a connected mountain, lake, wetland, and recreation landscape; it should not be confused with an unrestricted nature reserve or an all-weather watersports venue.

If you continue to the Yangtze riverfront, remain behind barriers and respect flood-control closures. Do not enter reed beds, feed birds, or pursue wildlife. Fog, storms, high water, heat, and maintenance can change safe routes.

## Practical planning

- Save 池州站, 杏花村文化旅游区, 齐山, and 平天湖 in Chinese.
- Confirm trains on the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/).
- Recheck any tourism bus against current transport notices rather than relying on an older route announcement.
- Carry water, rain protection, insect repellent, your passport, and a payment backup.
- Check the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) forecast before lake, hill, or riverfront walks.

For sacred-mountain travel, use a separate [Jiuhuashan guide](/guides/jiuhuashan-two-day-cultural-mountain-guide/) and allow enough time for its own transport and access rules.
