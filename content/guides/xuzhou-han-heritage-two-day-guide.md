Xuzhou is one of eastern China's strongest archaeology stops, especially for travelers who want to understand the Han dynasty beyond a textbook timeline. Royal tombs, jade, stone reliefs, and smaller terracotta figures are distributed among several sites rather than gathered in one attraction.

Spend two days and organize the trip by geography: one day around the Han Culture Scenic Area and one around Xuzhou Museum plus a second tomb or city landscape. Do not assume every site called a “Han tomb” is in the same park.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** archaeology, Han dynasty history, museums, and travelers moving between Beijing and the lower Yangtze.
- **Time needed:** two full days.
- **Simple plan:** Lion Mountain area on day one; Xuzhou Museum and one additional site on day two.
- **Main planning issue:** collections and tombs are spread across the city, with different entry arrangements.
- **Editorial view:** see the museum before judging the burial sites only by scale.

## Day one: focus on the Lion Mountain group

Build the day around the Han Culture Scenic Area, associated with the Tomb of the King of Chu at Lion Mountain and the Museum of Han Terracotta Warriors. The figures are much smaller than Xi'an's Qin warriors and belong to a different historical setting; comparison is useful, but calling them a miniature copy misses their own funerary and political context.

Allow time for both objects and landscape. Underground or enclosed spaces may have narrow passages, steps, humidity, and visitor limits. Ask staff which components are open that day, because conservation or maintenance can change the usable route.

Return to the center rather than adding a distant tomb late in the afternoon. A lakeside walk around Yunlong Lake is a flexible finish, especially after several hours indoors.

## Day two: museum first, then choose one extension

Start at Xuzhou Museum. Its complex includes material connected with Han archaeology as well as later periods, helping distinguish royal burial goods from the wider history of the city. Check the official museum channel for reservations, passport acceptance, opening days, temporary exhibitions, and gallery closures.

For the afternoon, choose one additional archaeological site, such as Guishan Han Tomb, only after confirming access and travel time. Rock-cut tombs reward slow observation, but the physical environment may not suit every visitor. Travelers who prefer an easier day can stay central for architecture, food, and another section of Yunlong Lake.

Avoid buying jade or “excavated” antiquities on the strength of a sales story. Museum objects provide context; they do not authenticate souvenirs.

## Rail and city logistics

Xuzhou is a major railway junction, but Xuzhou Railway Station and Xuzhou East Railway Station serve different trains and are not interchangeable. Search the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/) using the exact date, then map the named station to your hotel.

Attractions can be far apart even when English maps group them under “Xuzhou Han culture.” Save the Chinese names, show the exact destination to the driver, and avoid stacking two remote tombs around a fixed train departure.

## Practical checklist

- Save Xuzhou as 徐州, Lion Mountain as 狮子山, and Xuzhou Museum as 徐州博物馆.
- Carry the passport used for any reservation.
- Confirm which galleries and underground spaces are open.
- Wear shoes with grip for stone surfaces and steps.
- Use one archaeological cluster per half-day.
- Verify the exact rail station before departure.
