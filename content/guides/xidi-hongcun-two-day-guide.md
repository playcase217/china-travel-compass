Xidi and Hongcun are often bundled into one hurried day, but they are more rewarding as an overnight heritage trip. Both preserve southern Anhui village architecture, clan spaces, narrow lanes, and carefully managed water, yet they do not feel identical.

Spend one substantial half-day in each village and stay nearby. Hongcun is the more immediately recognizable landscape, centered on water and mountain views; Xidi rewards slower attention to lanes, residences, gateways, and spatial detail.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** architecture, village history, photography, sketching, and Huangshan travelers.
- **Time needed:** two days and one night; a long day is possible but compressed.
- **Simple plan:** Hongcun on day one, Xidi on day two, with the order adjusted to transport.
- **Main planning issue:** regional transfers are less simple than the map suggests.
- **Best pace:** early and late walks, with a quieter indoor or meal break during peak hours.

## Why the villages matter

UNESCO recognizes Xidi and Hongcun for the survival of their street plans, architecture, decoration, and integrated water systems. Their form reflects the society and trading wealth of historic Huizhou, where successful officials and merchants helped shape elaborate family and village environments.

The villages are not open-air film sets. They remain communities as well as protected visitor destinations. That tension—between lived space, commerce, conservation, and tourism—is part of what you should notice.

## Day one: read Hongcun through water

Begin around Hongcun’s water system, then move away from the most photographed viewpoints into side lanes. The pond and waterways are not decorative extras; they help explain the village plan and its relationship to the surrounding landscape.

Visit interiors that are officially open, but do not assume every doorway welcomes visitors. Details in timber frames, courtyards, rooflines, and carved decoration become easier to understand when you stop comparing the village to a checklist of photo locations.

If staying nearby, walk again late in the day when day groups thin. Follow tripod, drone, and commercial-photography rules posted locally.

## Day two: give Xidi its own rhythm

Xidi’s appeal is cumulative. Walk through its lanes slowly, look back at gateways and rooflines, and notice how houses, walls, and small open spaces guide movement. Allow time for interiors and interpretation instead of treating it as a shorter version of Hongcun.

Keep the afternoon flexible for the regional transfer. Weather, holiday crowds, and road traffic can all affect departures. If you must connect to a long-distance train, leave a generous buffer or stay closer to the station the previous night.

## Getting there and between villages

The wider Huangshan region includes multiple transport hubs and similarly named destinations. Search China Railway 12306 for your actual date and verify the exact station. Then confirm the road transfer to your village separately with your accommodation, an official visitor desk, or a reputable transport provider.

Do not assume a bus timetable found in an old blog still applies. Ask where the vehicle actually departs, whether advance booking is required, and what the last practical departure is. Ride-hailing availability may be uneven at busy or late hours.

## Where to stay

An overnight near one of the villages gives you the best light and a calmer experience. Before paying, ask the property to confirm your exact room, luggage access, cancellation terms, and ability to complete foreign-guest registration. Historic buildings may have stairs, thin walls, small bathrooms, or limited vehicle access.

Travel light if your lodging is inside a pedestrian lane. Keep the Chinese address and a contact number available offline.

## Respectful photography and visiting

- Ask before photographing residents at close range.
- Do not enter homes or courtyards unless they are clearly open to visitors.
- Keep doorways and narrow lanes passable.
- Follow rules for drones, tripods, flash, and commercial shoots.
- Do not touch carvings, furniture, or ancestral objects.
- Buy from local businesses without treating residents as scenery.

## How to combine the villages with Huangshan

Xidi and Hongcun complement rather than replace the mountain. Allow separate days for the villages and for Huangshan’s summit area, with a weather buffer if the mountain is your priority. Our [Huangshan two-day guide](/guides/huangshan-two-day-guide/) explains the physical demands and route choices.

For a wider Anhui trip, add the villages before or after Huangshan instead of squeezing them into a summit transfer day.
