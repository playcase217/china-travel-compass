Anyang is the trip to make when the names “Shang dynasty” and “oracle bones” deserve more than a museum label. The UNESCO-listed archaeological site of Yinxu lies on both sides of the Huan River and includes separate palace-and-ancestral-shrine and royal-tomb areas, so it should be treated as a landscape rather than one gallery.

Give Anyang two days: use the first for the principal Yinxu museum and ruins, then add the royal tombs or another city museum on day two. The site is still an active conservation environment, and opening arrangements can change.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** archaeology, early Chinese writing, bronze culture, and museum-focused travel.
- **Time needed:** two full days.
- **Simple plan:** museum and palace area first; tombs and city context second.
- **Main planning issue:** similarly named Yinxu components are not all at one entrance.
- **Editorial view:** understand the writing and excavation evidence before chasing individual objects.

## Day one: build the historical framework

Start with the current Yinxu Museum and the palace-and-ancestral-shrine area. The museum gives essential context for inscribed oracle bones, bronze vessels, burial practice, and the archaeological methods used to reconstruct the late Shang capital.

Do not assume an English translation is available for every label. Download background reading in advance, save the Chinese name 殷墟博物馆, and allow more time than a conventional city museum. Ask staff which ruins and galleries are included in your ticket and which entrance to use.

After the museum, walk only within the sections officially open to visitors. Excavated foundations can look visually modest; their value lies in the evidence and spatial relationships explained by the displays.

## Day two: royal tombs or broader Anyang

If open and practical on your date, continue to the royal-tomb area. It is a separate component of the UNESCO property, so confirm admission, transfer time, and the last entry before leaving your hotel. Avoid squeezing it between a late arrival and a fixed train.

Alternatively, use day two for another official museum in Anyang and a slower city walk. This is the better choice during severe heat, winter weather, or conservation closures. Fu Hao's tomb and objects associated with her are central to many accounts of Yinxu, but visitors should distinguish the archaeological location from objects displayed in different venues.

## Rail and local logistics

Anyang Railway Station and Anyang East Railway Station are different. Search your exact date on the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/) and map the printed station name before booking a hotel or transfer.

- Carry the passport used for reservations and tickets.
- Confirm the museum's opening day and passport booking procedure directly.
- Save Yinxu as 殷墟 and Anyang as 安阳.
- Budget separate transfers between site components.
- Wear shoes suitable for outdoor archaeological paths.
- Recheck last entry rather than relying on an aggregator.

## How to continue

Continue south to the Buddhist sculpture of [Longmen Grottoes](/guides/luoyang-longmen-grottoes-guide/) or east to the Song-dynasty city story in [Kaifeng](/guides/kaifeng-two-day-itinerary/). Our [high-speed rail guide](/guides/china-high-speed-rail-guide/) explains passport-based rail planning.
