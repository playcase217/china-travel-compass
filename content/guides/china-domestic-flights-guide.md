Domestic flights are most useful when China's geography makes rail slow or indirect. Book with the exact passport you will carry, compare the actual airports and ground transfers, and read the operating airline's current baggage and battery rules before packing.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** long cross-country journeys, remote gateways, and routes without a practical direct train.
- **Book carefully:** match the passport name and document number exactly.
- **Check the airport:** many cities have more than one, and a connection may involve a terminal or airport change.
- **Baggage:** allowances and fees depend on the airline, fare, route, and aircraft.
- **Power banks:** domestic-flight rules include current CCC-mark and recall restrictions; verify with CAAC and the airline immediately before travel.

## Compare the whole journey

A short flight can still consume most of a day after travel to the airport, check-in, security, boarding, baggage reclaim, and the destination transfer. Compare door-to-door time with the [China high-speed rail guide](/guides/china-high-speed-rail-guide/), not flight time alone.

Check the airport name and code before payment. Beijing, Shanghai, Chengdu, and other large cities have multiple airports or terminals. A cheap fare can become poor value if it requires a long transfer, a very early departure, or an unprotected self-connection.

## Booking with a passport

The central government's visitor guidance says foreign travelers can book through airline websites, apps, mini programs, or ticketing platforms. Enter the passenger name in the format required by the channel, but make sure spelling, passport number, nationality, and document validity match the passport used at check-in.

Save the airline record locator, ticket number, flight number, operating carrier, airport, terminal, and seller's support channel. If a third party issues the ticket, changes and refunds may need to go back through that seller.

## Check-in, security, and boarding

CAAC passenger-service rules require the valid identity document used for purchase at check-in. Airlines and airports set deadlines, so consult their current notice and arrive with enough time for document checks, baggage drop, security, and a possibly distant gate.

Keep the original passport accessible. Recheck the departure display after security because gates and boarding times can change. Do not plan a tight same-day attraction reservation after a flight.

## Baggage and batteries

CAAC advises passengers to check the carrier's published rules for checked and cabin baggage size, weight, quantity, fees, special items, and liability. Do not rely on an allowance from a different airline or an earlier trip.

Keep valuables and travel documents with you. Spare lithium batteries and power banks must not be placed in checked baggage, and energy limits or carrier approval may apply. CAAC announced that, from June 28, 2025, power banks without a CCC mark, with an unclear CCC mark, or from recalled models or batches are prohibited on domestic flights. Because battery policy can change quickly, review the newest CAAC and airline notice before leaving for the airport.

Ask the airline in advance about wheelchairs, medical devices, medication, sports equipment, instruments, animals, or unusual baggage. Security staff—not a social-media post—make the operational decision at the airport.

## Changes, delays, and connections

Fare conditions determine voluntary changes and refunds. For disruptions, monitor the operating airline and airport, keep receipts, and ask the responsible carrier what assistance applies to this flight. CAAC rules provide complaint channels, but they do not make every delay eligible for the same remedy.

For separate tickets, assume you may need to collect baggage, enter the public arrivals area, move terminals or airports, check in again, and clear security again. Build a conservative buffer or choose a protected through-ticket.
