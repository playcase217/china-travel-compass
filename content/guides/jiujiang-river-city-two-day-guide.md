Jiujiang is often reduced to the gateway for Lushan, but the river city has its own coherent story. Under its older name, Xunyang, it appears across Chinese history and literature; its modern waterfront, museums, towers, and lakes reveal a port shaped by the Yangtze. Two days let you stay in the city rather than squeezing it around a mountain transfer.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** literary associations, river-city history, museums, urban walks, and slower Jiangxi travel.
- **Time needed:** two days in Jiujiang itself.
- **Simple plan:** museum and lakes; Xunyang riverfront and historic landmarks.
- **Main planning issue:** distinguishing the city itinerary from Lushan and Poyang Lake excursions.
- **Editorial view:** do not treat Jiujiang as leftover time after Lushan.

## Day one: museum context and the city's lakes

Begin at a confirmed-open municipal museum or cultural complex. Use it to understand the relationship between the Yangtze, Poyang Lake system, trade, literature, and the city's changing urban form. Official city planning identifies Jiujiang Museum and its cultural complex as anchors for interpreting local history and intangible heritage.

In the afternoon, walk an established public route around 甘棠湖 (Gantang Lake) and nearby city streets. 烟水亭 (Yanshui Pavilion) may be part of the circuit if it is open, but separate the site's documented history from later literary storytelling. Leave time for everyday street life rather than stacking distant landmarks.

## Day two: follow the Xunyang riverfront

Build the second day around a compact riverside sequence such as 浔阳楼 (Xunyang Tower), 锁江楼 (Suojing Tower), and another confirmed-open waterfront site. Jiujiang's own historical account notes that the surviving Suojing pagoda is old while the accompanying tower was reconstructed in 2002—an important distinction in a city where literary memory and physical fabric overlap.

Walk only on open promenades and obey flood, construction, and navigation barriers. The Yangtze is working infrastructure, not a casual swimming or scrambling area. If river levels or weather close a path, move inland rather than detouring around fencing.

For Lushan, use a separate plan: the mountain requires transport, weather, and trail decisions of its own. See the [Lushan first-time guide](/guides/lushan-two-day-first-time-guide/).

## Practical planning

- Distinguish 九江站, 庐山站, and other regional stations when booking.
- Check services through the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/).
- Save 浔阳楼, 锁江楼, 烟水亭, and 九江市博物馆 in Chinese.
- Carry your passport and verify reservation or identification rules for each venue.
- Check the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) and local flood or riverfront notices.
- Do not assume evening performances, cruises, or extended museum hours operate every day.
