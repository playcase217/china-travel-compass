Yixing is often introduced through purple-clay teapots, but a useful visit connects finished objects with clay, kilns, makers, neighborhoods, and the greener landscape south of the city. It is not necessary—or wise—to begin with an expensive purchase.

Use the first day for ceramics and the Dingshu area. On the second, choose one nature experience such as the bamboo sea or a managed cave, allowing weather and mobility to decide.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** ceramics, craft processes, tea ware, workshops, bamboo, and a quieter Jiangsu stop.
- **Time needed:** two full days.
- **Simple plan:** museum and old kiln streets; then one southern Yixing nature area.
- **Main planning issue:** authenticity claims, workshop reservations, dispersed sites, and holiday traffic.
- **Editorial view:** learn before buying; one modest object with clear provenance beats a speculative “masterpiece.”

## Day one: understand the pottery landscape

Start with a confirmed-open ceramics museum or institutional collection. Check its closed day, passport acceptance, reservation rules, and whether English interpretation is available. Focus on how vessel form, firing, clay preparation, use, and changing markets relate.

Continue to a managed old street or creative district in Dingshu. Workshops are workplaces: ask before entering, photographing, touching clay, or joining an activity. Book hands-on sessions directly with a clearly identified operator and confirm what is included, how long firing takes, and whether completed work can actually be collected or shipped.

For zisha shopping, distrust guaranteed investment returns, vague “master” attribution, and unsupported claims about rare clay. Request the maker or studio name, material description, dimensions, price, and receipt in writing. Do not assume a high price proves age or authorship.

## Day two: choose bamboo or cave, not both

Select Yixing Bamboo Sea for a landscape-led day or Shanjuan Cave for a more contained geological attraction. They have different walking surfaces and transport needs. Confirm admission, operating components, weather restrictions, last entry, and return transport before leaving town.

In bamboo country, remain on marked paths and be cautious on wet steps. In a cave, expect damp surfaces, steps, temperature changes, and possibly confined sections; optional rides or performances may not operate every day. Never bypass a closed trail or cave passage.

If heavy rain, heat, or holiday congestion makes the outing impractical, stay with pottery: another museum, a pre-booked studio, or a public old-street walk forms a complete second day.

## Getting there and planning the stay

- Check trains through the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/).
- Save 宜兴 for Yixing and 丁蜀镇 for Dingshu Town.
- Map the station, pottery district, accommodation, and chosen nature site before booking.
- Allow extra time for south-Yixing road travel on weekends and holidays.

Yixing pairs well with [Wuxi](/guides/wuxi-taihu-two-day-itinerary/) or [Nanjing](/guides/nanjing-two-day-itinerary/), but the craft district deserves an overnight rather than a shopping detour.
