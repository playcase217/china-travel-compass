English speakers can rent an apartment in China, but the process is easier when you treat it as a document-and-verification task, not just a housing search. Confirm who has the legal right to rent the apartment, get a written lease, understand the deposit and payment schedule, and complete temporary residence registration after moving in.

## At a glance

Use a reputable platform, licensed agency, employer, school, or trusted local contact to find housing. Visit the apartment before paying. Verify the landlord's identity, property ownership, and any sublease authorization. Do not transfer large payments before signing a clear contract. After moving into a private apartment, foreign residents generally need to complete temporary residence registration within 24 hours.

## Who this guide is for

This guide is for English-speaking visitors who plan to stay longer than a normal hotel trip: students, employees, family visitors, digital nomads, language learners, and travelers taking an extended stay in one city.

Short-term visitors may be better served by hotels or serviced apartments because those properties usually handle guest registration. If you rent a private apartment, you take on more responsibility for paperwork, communication, and local compliance.

## Where to look for apartments

The best starting point depends on your situation:

- **Employer or school housing office:** often the safest option for first-time arrivals.
- **Reputable real-estate agencies:** useful when you need neighborhood guidance and bilingual help.
- **Long-stay serviced apartments:** more expensive, but simpler for registration and billing.
- **Local rental platforms:** useful after you understand the city and can verify listings carefully.
- **Expat community referrals:** helpful, but still require normal document checks.

Avoid making decisions from photos alone. Online listings can be outdated, duplicated, or represented by multiple agents.

## Use an agent carefully

Agents can save time, especially if you do not read Chinese fluently. A good agent can arrange viewings, explain neighborhoods, and help coordinate the lease.

Before relying on an agent, ask:

- Which company they represent.
- Whether they can provide an official invoice or agency agreement.
- Who pays the agency fee and how much it is.
- Whether they will help with residence registration paperwork.
- Whether the landlord or property owner will be present for signing.

Do not let speed pressure replace verification. A common mistake is paying a deposit because an agent says the apartment will disappear immediately.

## Verify the right person can rent the apartment

Before paying, check that the person signing the lease has the right to rent the apartment. Ask to see:

- The landlord's identity document.
- Property ownership documentation.
- If the signer is not the owner, written authorization to rent or sublet.
- The apartment address matching the documents.
- A clear list of included furniture, appliances, and utilities.

If your Chinese is limited, have a trusted bilingual person review the documents. For higher-value leases, consider professional legal help.

## Understand the lease contract

Get the lease in writing. If there is an English translation, make sure you know which language controls in a dispute. Many contracts are ultimately Chinese-language contracts, even when an English explanation is provided.

Key terms to confirm:

- Exact apartment address.
- Names and identity details of both parties.
- Lease term and move-in date.
- Monthly rent and payment schedule.
- Deposit amount and refund conditions.
- Agency fee, if any.
- Who pays utilities, internet, heating, property management, and repair costs.
- Rules on early termination.
- Inventory of furniture and appliances.
- Responsibility for residence registration support.

Photograph the apartment condition on move-in day, including meters, appliances, walls, floors, and existing damage.

## Deposits and payment schedules

Payment patterns vary by city and landlord. Some leases require one month's deposit and several months of rent upfront. Others use a different rhythm.

Before sending money:

- Confirm the recipient's name matches the contract or authorized party.
- Avoid cash payments unless you receive a clear receipt.
- Keep payment screenshots and bank records.
- Understand exactly when the deposit can be withheld.
- Do not pay a large amount before seeing the apartment and signing the contract.

Set up payment apps before you need them. The [China apps guide for English speakers](/guides/china-apps-for-english-speakers/) explains how to prepare Alipay, WeChat, DiDi, and other practical apps.

## Temporary residence registration

Foreigners staying in China need to register their place of stay. Hotels usually handle this automatically. If you live in a private apartment, you or the landlord may need to complete temporary residence registration with the local public security office or approved online system.

In many cases, this should be completed within 24 hours after moving in. Requirements can vary by city and district, so confirm the local process before signing a lease. Ask the landlord or agent whether they will cooperate with registration. If they refuse, treat that as a serious warning sign.

You may need your passport, visa or residence permit information, lease, landlord details, and property documents.

## Neighborhood and building checks

A cheaper apartment is not always cheaper after transport, utilities, and daily inconvenience.

Check:

- Commute time during realistic hours.
- Distance to subway or bus routes.
- Elevator availability if you have luggage or mobility needs.
- Heating and air-conditioning setup.
- Internet installation options.
- Noise from roads, bars, construction, or neighbors.
- Whether the building accepts foreign residents and supports registration.
- Nearby groceries, pharmacies, and basic services.

For Beijing transport planning, read the [Beijing subway guide](/guides/beijing-subway-guide/).

## Language and communication

Many rental interactions happen in Chinese. Prepare translated questions in advance and keep communication in writing when possible.

Useful habits:

- Save the apartment address in Chinese.
- Ask the agent to summarize key terms in a message.
- Screenshot important promises before signing.
- Use translation tools, but do not rely on machine translation for legal terms.
- Ask for receipts, not just verbal confirmation.

## Common rental risks

Watch for these red flags:

- The landlord refuses to show ownership or authorization documents.
- The agent asks for a large deposit before a viewing.
- The lease does not match the apartment address.
- The apartment cannot support foreigner registration.
- Fees are explained only verbally.
- The rent seems far below comparable apartments.
- The landlord refuses receipts for payments.
- You are pushed to sign immediately without reading the contract.

If something feels unclear, slow down. Losing a good apartment is better than entering a bad lease.

## A simple rental checklist

Before signing:

- View the apartment in person or through a trusted representative.
- Verify landlord identity and property rights.
- Confirm the apartment can be registered for a foreign tenant.
- Review the full lease and payment schedule.
- Photograph the apartment condition.
- Confirm utility and internet responsibilities.
- Keep copies of all documents and payments.

After moving in:

- Complete temporary residence registration.
- Save emergency and property-management contacts.
- Record meter readings.
- Test appliances and report problems quickly.
- Keep the lease and registration record accessible.

Renting in China is manageable for English speakers, but it rewards patience and documentation. Treat every step as part of the same process: find the apartment, verify the right to rent it, sign clearly, pay traceably, and register your stay properly.
