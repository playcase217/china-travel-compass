Xinyang sits where Henan meets the landscapes of the Dabie Mountains, making it a convincing tea destination as well as a useful rail stop. Two days can pair a careful introduction to Xinyang Maojian with Jigong Mountain, whose forest scenery and early modern villa district tell a very different story from the tea hills.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** green tea, forest walks, mountain air, early modern architecture, and a slower regional itinerary.
- **Time needed:** two days, with one day centered on tea and one devoted to Jigong Mountain.
- **Simple plan:** tea museum, market, or verified producer; then a full mountain day.
- **Main planning issue:** tea gardens, central-city venues, and the mountain are separate clusters, while seasonal buses and visitor routes can change.
- **Editorial view:** one transparent, educational tea visit is better than a rushed circuit of sales rooms.

## Day one: learn how Xinyang Maojian is made

Begin at a confirmed-open tea museum, cultural venue, or reputable producer that explains leaf grades, picking seasons, pan-firing, rolling, and brewing. Xinyang Maojian is a protected regional green tea, but the words “Maojian” or “spring tea” alone do not establish origin, harvest date, or quality. Ask for the producer, grade, packing date, and a receipt before buying.

If you visit a plantation, arrange it directly with a farm, cooperative, or licensed operator. Tea picking is agricultural work, not a guaranteed visitor activity, and access depends on harvest timing, rain, farm operations, and the host's permission. Stay on designated paths, do not handle equipment without invitation, and never assume that a promotional photo includes a tasting or workshop.

Use the rest of the day for an ordinary local meal and a tea shop where staff will brew before selling. Taste first, buy modestly, and avoid health claims or pressure to purchase expensive gift boxes.

## Day two: Jigong Mountain

鸡公山 (Jigong Mountain) deserves most of a day. The protected scenic area combines forest, ridgelines, viewpoints, and a notable collection of villas associated with its development as a summer retreat. Read the buildings as layered heritage rather than a single intact “foreign town”: some structures have been restored or adapted, and individual interiors may not be accessible.

Choose the official visitor route that fits current weather and mobility. Mountain fog, rain, ice, heat, fire controls, construction, and crowd limits can close trails or viewpoints. Do not enter abandoned-looking buildings, shortcut through woodland, or treat a quiet service road as a public trail.

## Practical planning

- Check the exact Xinyang station and current trains on [China Railway 12306](https://www.12306.cn/en/).
- Confirm Jigong Mountain reservations, entrances, internal transport, last departures, and trail status through current official notices.
- Carry the passport used for bookings and allow time for manual registration.
- Ask tea hosts what is included and whether English interpretation is available before paying.
- Check the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) for mountain storms, fog, heat, cold, and fire-risk conditions.
- Treat spring tea events, night tours, shuttles, performances, and harvest experiences as seasonal and changeable.
