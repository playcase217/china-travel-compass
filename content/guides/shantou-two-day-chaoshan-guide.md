Shantou is the best city base for understanding how trade, migration, overseas-Chinese investment, food, and everyday Chaoshan culture meet. A first visit should not try to cover Shantou, Chaozhou, Jieyang, and Nan'ao Island at once.

Spend one day in the Small Park opening-port district and one day on a museum-and-waterfront route. Make meals part of the plan, but leave room for context beyond restaurant checklists.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** port history, arcade streets, food culture, and travelers continuing through eastern Guangdong.
- **Time needed:** two full days.
- **Simple plan:** Small Park and the old commercial port; then a cultural venue and one waterfront district.
- **Main planning issue:** summer heat, holiday crowds, scattered attractions, and restaurant queues.
- **Editorial view:** Shantou deserves its own city stay; Nan'ao Island is a separate excursion.

## Day one: read the old commercial port

Start around the Small Park historic district, where radial streets, arcades, former commercial buildings, religious spaces, and old public institutions preserve the shape of the treaty-port-era city. Use an official visitor map and choose only museums or interiors confirmed open that day.

Walk slowly enough to notice how commerce and housing share the same streets. Restoration continues in parts of the district, so obey barriers and avoid entering apparently empty buildings. Some lanes remain part of local daily life: do not block doors or photograph residents closely without permission.

Parts of the district are subject to current time-based and all-day motor-vehicle restrictions. Treat the old city as a walking visit, check your drop-off point before departure, and expect some bus routing to change on public holidays.

Plan lunch outside the busiest minute. For beef hotpot, seafood, rice-noodle rolls, or *gue* snacks, ask the price and portion before ordering. Raw or lightly marinated seafood carries food-safety risk; choose a reputable, busy establishment and select cooked dishes if you are uncertain.

## Day two: culture first, waterfront second

Choose one confirmed-open cultural stop, such as a municipal museum or the Chaoshan Historical and Cultural Expo Center, and verify its closed day, reservation method, accepted identity document, and final entry. This gives useful context for the region's language, opera, crafts, migration, and foodways.

Then select one waterfront area rather than crossing the city repeatedly. Weather and visibility should determine the route. Coastal wind, intense sun, thunderstorms, typhoons, and temporary seafront controls can make a planned promenade unsuitable.

Do not bolt Nan'ao Island onto a half-day. Bridge traffic, island transfers, beach conditions, and coastal weather deserve a separate full-day plan with a generous return buffer.

## Arrival and practical planning

- Check the exact station and train on the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/).
- Save 汕头 for Shantou and 小公园开埠区 for the Small Park opening-port district.
- Keep a written hotel address for taxi or ride-hailing pickup.
- Carry water and sun protection, but use indoor breaks during the hottest part of the day.

Shantou pairs naturally with [Chaozhou](/guides/chaozhou-two-day-old-city-guide/), but stay overnight in each city if you want both without rushing.
