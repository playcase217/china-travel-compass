Longnan's Wudu District is a mountain-city base for one of southeastern Gansu's most accessible geological sights: Wanxiang Cave. Two days give you room to arrive, understand the Bailong River valley, and visit the cave without gambling on a same-day long connection through changeable mountain weather.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** caves, geology, mountain-city scenery, and an overland Gansu route.
- **Time needed:** two days, including arrival margin.
- **Simple plan:** settle into Wudu and learn the valley layout; visit Wanxiang Cave on day two.
- **Main planning issue:** road, reservation, and scenic-vehicle arrangements can change, especially after severe weather.
- **Editorial view:** the cave is the main event; do not dilute the trip with distant Longnan counties that need separate days.

## Day one: understand Wudu's geography

Use the first day to arrive in 武都区, verify the next day's booking, and orient yourself around the Bailong River valley. Wudu's built-up area, steep surrounding slopes, and roads through narrow terrain make map distances deceptive. Keep to public urban paths and respect any flood-control, slope-stabilization, or construction closures.

Choose an accommodation that confirms it accepts foreign guests and can clearly identify the pickup point for your legal taxi, hired car, or other transport. Ask the property or official visitor service to write the cave visitor center in Chinese rather than relying on a loosely placed English map pin.

Wanxiang Street or another current public evening area can be an optional walk, not a guaranteed performance or event. Check what is actually open and remain aware of traffic on narrow streets.

## Day two: visit Wanxiang Cave

The Wudu government places 万象洞 in Hanwang Town, outside the central city; see its [official attraction introduction](https://zwfw.gansu.gov.cn/longnan/lnsfwyy/wdqlyjdcx/art/2024/art_c094fd180f9b4bbbae3d3c5c56366437.html). The district's [official reservation notice](https://zwfw.gansu.gov.cn/wudu/zczx/tzgg/art/2023/art_2c79670d453b4f198a012a2eaf4e8217.html) describes real-name online booking and a transfer from the visitor center by scenic vehicle. Treat those instructions as a starting point and verify the current process, accepted passport entry, price, and vehicle arrangement before travel.

Inside, use handrails, wear shoes with reliable grip, and expect damp surfaces, steps, low light, and temperature differences. Do not touch formations, leave the route, use prohibited flash or tripods, or scratch names into rock. Travelers with limited mobility, balance concerns, claustrophobia, or respiratory issues should ask the venue about stairs, route length, exits, and assistance before booking.

Allow a large buffer for the return. Heavy rain, landslide risk, road works, crowd controls, and scenic-vehicle operations can change the day. If officials close the road or cave, do not try an informal track or alternate entrance.

## Connections and sensible extensions

Verify any rail leg on the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/) and all bus or road legs with the operating station or carrier. Save 陇南站, 武都区, 万象洞游客中心, and 汉王镇 in Chinese. “Longnan” can describe the entire prefecture, so confirm that a hotel or attraction is actually in Wudu before booking.

For a separate northern extension, see our [Tianshui and Maijishan guide](/guides/tianshui-maijishan-two-day-guide/). For mountain landscapes farther south, the [Jiuzhaigou first-time guide](/guides/jiuzhaigou-first-time-guide/) needs its own transport and accommodation plan; it is not a casual add-on to Wanxiang Cave.

Use the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) and local emergency notices for heavy rain, thunderstorms, snow, heat, floods, and geological-hazard warnings.
