Zhaoqing offers an unusually clean two-day pairing in Guangdong: lakeside limestone peaks at Seven Star Crags and humid forest at Dinghu Mountain. They belong to the wider Xinghu scenic area but should be treated as separate day plans.

Base in urban Zhaoqing, spend one day around the crags, then give Dinghu Mountain its own weather-aware morning and afternoon. This is a restorative alternative to adding another dense city stop to a Pearl River Delta route.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** easy landscape walks, subtropical forest, photography, and a break from major-city pacing.
- **Time needed:** two full days.
- **Simple plan:** Seven Star Crags and lake edges; Dinghu Mountain forest and cultural sites.
- **Main planning issue:** heat, rain, slippery paths, and understanding separate entrances or transport zones.
- **Editorial view:** choose fewer paths and spend longer reading the landscape.

## Day one: Seven Star Crags

Begin with an official map and select one coherent circuit. Guangdong's foreign affairs office identifies Seven Star Crags and Dinghu Mountain together as the Xinghu Scenic and Historic Interest Area, but the crags themselves form a lake-and-limestone landscape in Zhaoqing's Duanzhou District.

Walk the lake edges before deciding whether to add caves, boats, or elevated viewpoints. Those extras may have separate admission, queues, operating hours, or weather restrictions. Confirm them at the official entrance instead of buying an unclear bundle in advance.

Avoid climbing closed rock, touching inscriptions, feeding wildlife, or stepping into planted and wetland areas. After heavy rain, accept closures and rerouting. A slower circuit in good light is more rewarding than hurrying across every bridge.

## Day two: Dinghu Mountain

Dinghushan is not merely a city park. UNESCO describes it as a biosphere reserve of low mountains and humid subtropical and tropical forest, designated in 1979. It also contains longstanding Buddhist cultural associations.

Start early, check the day's open trails and internal transport, and choose a route suited to your footwear and the heat. Carry water, but do not treat natural springs as automatically safe drinking water. Stay on marked paths, keep sound low, and never collect plants, rocks, or insects.

Temple or religious areas require ordinary courtesy: dress respectfully, avoid interrupting worship, and follow photography restrictions. Conservation access and religious access can change independently, so verify both.

## Weather and practical planning

Southern Guangdong can be hot, humid, and intensely wet. Rain changes path conditions quickly, while thunderstorms and typhoons can affect transport and close outdoor areas. Use the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) for official forecasts and heed local warnings.

Bring shoes with grip, quick-drying clothing, water, and mosquito protection. In hot weather, reduce the route rather than pushing through symptoms of heat illness.

## Getting there and onward

- Check rail options on the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/).
- Verify the exact Zhaoqing station on the ticket; stations with similar destination branding are not interchangeable.
- Use official maps or your lodging to confirm the correct scenic-area entrance before taking a taxi.
- Pair Zhaoqing with [Guangzhou](/guides/guangzhou-two-day-itinerary/) or [Foshan](/guides/foshan-lingnan-culture-day-trip/) on separate days.
