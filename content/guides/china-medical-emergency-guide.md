For a medical emergency in mainland China, call 120 for an ambulance; call 110 for police and 119 for fire. If you can do so safely, involve a Chinese speaker, share a precise Chinese location, and contact your travel insurer. This guide is planning information, not medical diagnosis or treatment advice.

## At a glance

- **Ambulance and first aid:** 120.
- **Police:** 110. **Fire:** 119. **Traffic accidents:** 122.
- **Before travel:** buy suitable insurance, record medical conditions and medications, and save Chinese addresses for your accommodation and likely hospital.
- **During an emergency:** describe the location first, follow dispatcher instructions, and do not delay urgent care while comparing facilities or costs.

## Calling for urgent help

State the address, nearby landmark, phone number, number of patients, and what happened. A hotel, attraction, station, or restaurant employee may help call and explain the location. Keep the line open and follow the dispatcher's instructions.

Language support is not guaranteed. Prepare a short Chinese note with your name, date of birth, allergies, major conditions, current medications, emergency contact, insurer, and the sentence “Please call 120” (请拨打120). Translation tools can assist communication but should not replace a clinician or interpreter for consent and treatment decisions.

## Choosing care without delaying it

China has public and private medical facilities with different services, language capacity, registration systems, and payment arrangements. In a true emergency, use the appropriate emergency service. For a non-urgent problem, ask your insurer or an official local health channel to identify a licensed facility able to address the specific need.

Do not choose a clinic solely from an advertisement, map review, or driver recommendation. Confirm the facility name, department, opening status, and address. A large hospital can have several campuses and entrances.

## Insurance and payment preparation

Contact the insurer's assistance line as soon as practical, but do not let authorization calls delay life-saving care. Ask which hospitals are in network, whether direct billing is possible, what documentation is required, and how reimbursement works.

Carry multiple payment options. Deposits or payment before some services may be requested, and an overseas policy may not be billed directly. Keep itemized invoices, medical records, prescriptions, test results, and discharge papers. Costs and procedures vary; obtain current information from the facility and insurer rather than relying on a quoted example.

## Medications and records

Bring enough legally permitted medication for the trip in original labeled packaging, with a prescription or clinician's letter showing generic names and doses. Check import and aviation rules before departure, especially for controlled substances, injections, refrigerated medicines, and medical devices.

Do not buy an unfamiliar substitute based only on packaging color or a translated brand name. Show the clinician or pharmacist the generic ingredient and strength. In an emergency, disclose allergies, anticoagulants, insulin, heart medicines, pregnancy, implanted devices, and other information that could affect treatment.

## After treatment

Ask what warning signs require urgent return, how to take each medicine, and whether it is safe to travel. Obtain written records before leaving. If continuing to another city or flying, ask the treating clinician and carrier about fitness to travel and assistance; neither should be assumed.

For a crime, serious accident, hospitalization, or death, the traveler's embassy or consulate may explain consular assistance but does not replace police, clinicians, insurers, or payment arrangements.

## A pre-trip health file

- Insurance policy and 24-hour assistance number.
- Passport and visa copies stored securely.
- Emergency contact and embassy details.
- Conditions, allergies, medications, and generic drug names.
- Accommodation address in Chinese.
- A payment card plus a separate backup.
- Records needed for any mobility, sensory, or communication assistance.
