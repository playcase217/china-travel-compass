Weifang deserves more than an event-season stop. Its kite identity connects design, bamboo work, painting, woodblock-print traditions, and contemporary festival culture. Spend one day in the central museum and garden district, then use a second day for a carefully arranged craft visit in Yangjiabu or another accountable workshop.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** folk art, design, hands-on craft, small museums, and family travel.
- **Time needed:** two days, with a half-day transfer for a suburban craft venue.
- **Simple plan:** kite museum and Shihu Garden area; Yangjiabu or one verified craft workshop.
- **Main planning issue:** festival venues, museums, workshops, and demonstrations do not share one schedule.
- **Editorial view:** visit for the making process, not just a photograph of large festival kites.

## Day one: kites in the city

Start at the World Kite Museum or its current official successor after confirming opening hours, reservation method, passport entry, and gallery access. Look for structure, balance, bamboo preparation, painting, and regional forms rather than assuming every modern display represents the same historical tradition.

Continue to the Shihu Garden cultural area if its historic spaces and exhibitions are open. The surrounding craft district can provide useful context, but separate protected heritage, curated demonstrations, and ordinary retail. Ask before photographing makers, and agree on any workshop fee before participating.

## Day two: Yangjiabu and linked crafts

Arrange transport to Yangjiabu only after a museum, heritage center, or reputable workshop confirms current access. China's national intangible-cultural-heritage platform identifies the area with Weifang kite-making and explains its historical relationship with local woodblock New Year prints. A good visit should show process and materials, not merely move visitors through a shop.

Do not claim that a purchased object is handmade, made by a named inheritor, or produced with traditional materials unless the seller documents that claim. If you plan to fly a kite, use a designated open area in safe weather, far from roads, railways, airports, power lines, trees, and crowds. Follow local instructions and never use dangerous line materials.

The international kite festival is a separate, date-specific event. Do not assume the annual venue, dates, admission, or transport plan from an old article. Check the organizer and municipal notices for the year of travel.

## Transport and timing

Verify your train and exact station through the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/). For local travel, save 世界风筝博物馆, 十笏园, 杨家埠, and your return station in Chinese.

Wind is part of the city's kite culture but also a safety issue. Check wind, thunderstorms, heat, dust, and severe-weather alerts through the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) before any outdoor activity.
