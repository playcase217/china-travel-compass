Kashgar is not a quick extension from Urumqi. Far-western Xinjiang has its own scale, rhythms, languages, and Uyghur cultural context, and the Old City remains both a visitor area and a lived neighborhood. Three days provide a useful minimum: two in the city and one flexible day for a verified excursion.

Prioritize observation over a checklist. Walk the Old City at different times, use museums for context, eat patiently, and ask before photographing people or private homes.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** Silk Road history, Uyghur food and craft traditions, architecture, markets, and slow street exploration.
- **Time needed:** three days, excluding the long journey from eastern China.
- **Simple plan:** Old City orientation, museum and neighborhoods, then one flexible excursion.
- **Main planning issue:** distance, language, seasonal weather, and changing access or transport arrangements.
- **Editorial view:** Kashgar rewards cultural attention more than a race through staged photo opportunities.

## Day one: learn the Old City's shape

Start with an unhurried walk through the Kashgar Old City. The official regional description emphasizes that it is simultaneously a residential, commercial, food, and tourism district. Stay on public routes, respect signs and barriers, and never treat a doorway or courtyard as permission to enter.

Markets and workshops are easiest to appreciate when you ask before photographing a person or a production process. Bargain politely where it is normal, but do not turn a small purchase into a performance. Learn a few food names, check portion sizes, and be aware that dietary practices matter in many local businesses.

Public squares and religious sites can have access controls. Follow current posted rules, dress respectfully, and do not assume tourist entry is available during worship or special periods.

## Day two: add historical context

Choose the Kashgar Museum or another official cultural venue to place the oasis, trade routes, archaeology, and regional communities in a wider timeline. Check opening days, passport requirements, reservations, and exhibition access through the venue's current official channel.

Then explore a different public section of the city instead of repeating only the busiest lanes. Markets can move, rebuild, specialize, or operate differently by day. Ask your hotel to verify the current location and hours of the specific market you want; “Kashgar bazaar” is not precise enough for a driver.

## Day three: one verified excursion

Choose an excursion only after checking distance, road conditions, weather, permits or access rules, and the operator. Mountain and border-region routes are not interchangeable with a casual suburban day trip. Foreign travelers should ask the relevant local authority or a reputable licensed operator whether any destination has nationality, document, or permit restrictions at the time of travel.

If a safe, confirmed excursion does not fit, keep the third day in Kashgar. A second museum, neighborhood food crawl, park, or craft-focused visit is better than improvising a remote route from an old blog post.

## Arrival, documents, and communication

Flights and long-distance trains can sell out around holidays. Search [China Railway 12306](https://www.12306.cn/en/) with your passport and exact date, and verify flights with the airline. Carry your passport, accommodation details, and offline copies of essential bookings.

Mandarin may not solve every conversation, and English should not be assumed. Save destinations in Chinese, use a translation app that works with your connectivity setup, and confirm the return journey before leaving the city.

- Save Kashgar as 喀什 and Kashgar Old City as 喀什古城.
- Allow recovery time after a long arrival.
- Ask before photographing people, homes, workshops, or worship.
- Verify market locations and excursion access locally.
- Prepare for strong sun, dry air, and large temperature swings.
- Keep transport plans flexible around weather and controls.
