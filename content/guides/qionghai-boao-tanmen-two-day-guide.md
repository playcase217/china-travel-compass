Qionghai makes a strong two-day cultural coast trip when you pair **Bo'ao's river-and-sea landscape** with **Tanmen harbor and the China (Hainan) Museum of the South China Sea**. The result is more substantial than a beach break: it introduces maritime archaeology, fishing traditions, contemporary conference geography, and an actively changing coastline.

The three names matter. Qionghai is the city, Jiaji is its main urban center, and Bo'ao and Tanmen are separate coastal towns. Do not book a hotel or transport on the assumption that they are one walkable resort strip.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** maritime history, museums, fishing-harbor culture, tropical coastlines, and travelers using Hainan's eastern rail line.
- **Time needed:** two full days, ideally with a coastal base and arranged transfers.
- **Simple plan:** Bo'ao river mouth and public areas; Tanmen and the South China Sea Museum.
- **Main planning issue:** conference security, museum reservations, construction, typhoons, surf, and marine activities can alter access.
- **Editorial view:** the museum is the intellectual anchor; visit it before treating the harbor simply as a seafood stop.

## Day one: read Bo'ao's geography

Begin in Bo'ao by walking only in clearly public riverside and coastal areas. The meeting of the Wanquan River system and the sea gives the town its distinctive setting, while the Bo'ao Forum for Asia has produced controlled venues and event security zones. Public access to Dongyu Island or conference facilities should never be assumed.

Use official signs and staff directions, especially during major meetings. A ticket sold by a third party does not override security restrictions. Avoid drones near conference, transport, military, or sensitive coastal infrastructure.

Spend the later afternoon along a managed public section of coast. Do not interpret calm-looking water as safe swimming: currents, wind, heat, lightning, and seasonal storms can be serious. Swim only where lifeguards and current notices explicitly permit it, and leave the shoreline when warnings are raised.

## Day two: Tanmen and maritime history

Start at the **China (Hainan) Museum of the South China Sea** after checking its official reservation process, passport acceptance, open galleries, and bag rules. Give the museum several hours. Its themes include maritime history, natural ecology, underwater cultural heritage, and the maritime Silk Road; these displays provide essential context for the nearby working harbor.

Continue to public areas of **Tanmen fishing harbor**. Keep clear of loading operations, wet decks, ropes, machinery, and restricted quays. Ask before photographing fishers or boats. If choosing seafood, confirm the species, weight, unit price, cooking fee, and total before ordering; avoid protected wildlife and questionable souvenirs.

Treat “fishing,” “sea foraging,” diving, or boat trips as optional commercial activities requiring a licensed operator. Confirm the departure point, passenger insurance, life jackets, weather cancellation, environmental rules, and return time. Never walk onto reefs or tidal flats without local authorization and tide knowledge.

## Practical planning

- Search Qionghai and Bo'ao stations separately on [China Railway 12306](https://www.12306.cn/en/); choose based on your accommodation and confirmed transfer.
- Save 琼海, 嘉积, 博鳌, 潭门, and 中国（海南）南海博物馆 in Chinese.
- Carry your passport and allow time for museum and conference-area security.
- Check the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) and official local warnings for typhoons, heavy rain, lightning, heat, wind, and rough seas.
- Use reef-safe behavior: do not collect coral, shells with living animals, archaeological material, or wildlife products.
