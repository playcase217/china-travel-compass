Xingkai Lake, also known internationally as Lake Khanka, is a vast wetland and border-lake landscape east of Jixi. Its appeal comes from scale, birdlife, geology, and the Xinkailiu archaeological story—not from getting as close as possible to wildlife or the international boundary.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** wetlands, birdwatching, lake landscapes, archaeology, and slow nature travel.
- **Time needed:** two days after reaching Jixi or Mishan.
- **Simple plan:** city or Mishan context and transfer; then a full day in one officially open lake zone.
- **Main planning issue:** the shoreline is long, visitor areas are dispersed, and protected-area and border restrictions override map pins.
- **Editorial view:** choose Xinkailiu or another confirmed visitor zone and explore it properly rather than chasing distant viewpoints.

## Day one: arrive, orient, and choose one lake sector

Use Jixi, Mishan, or a verified accommodation near the lake as your base according to current transport. Visit a confirmed-open local museum or exhibition to understand coal-region history, wetland ecology, and the Xinkailiu archaeological culture before heading into the reserve landscape.

Confirm the next day's exact entrance, road, driver, opening status, and return. “Xingkai Lake” is not one compact attraction: municipal listings distinguish Xinkailiu, Dangbi Town, farm-based resorts, and other zones. Trying to combine several in one day can turn the visit into hours of driving.

## Day two: Xinkailiu and wetland observation

兴凯湖新开流景区 (Xingkai Lake Xinkailiu Scenic Area) brings together designated viewing platforms, wetland scenery, a natural-history museum, and the Xinkailiu archaeological site. The Jixi government describes the zone between the large and small lakes as important habitat on a major migratory route.

Stay on open boardwalks and platforms. Keep a long distance from birds, use quiet voices, and avoid playback calls, feeding, flash, drones, or entering reeds. Migration, ice breakup, flowers, water levels, and bird concentrations are natural events, never guaranteed calendar performances.

The lake is international border water. Do not approach patrol facilities, cross barriers, launch a boat, or photograph sensitive infrastructure. Swim or take a cruise only where the responsible operator and current safety notices explicitly permit it; wind, cold water, waves, algae, storms, and ice can make apparently calm shorelines dangerous.

## Practical planning

- Check current trains and the exact station on [China Railway 12306](https://www.12306.cn/en/).
- Arrange a licensed vehicle for lake transfers and agree on the return before departure.
- Carry your passport and comply with registration, border, reserve, and photography rules.
- Bring binoculars rather than trying to approach wildlife.
- Check the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) for wind, storms, cold, heat, snow, and ice conditions.
- Treat boats, beaches, boardwalks, museums, seasonal lodging, and food services as changeable.
