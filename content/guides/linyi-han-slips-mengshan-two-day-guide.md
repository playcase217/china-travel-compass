Linyi offers an unusually strong pairing: Han-dynasty manuscript archaeology in the city and the broad geological landscapes of Yimengshan outside it. Two days are enough only if you keep those halves distinct. Begin with the museum, then choose one geopark sector rather than treating the whole park as a single mountain.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** manuscript history, archaeology, museums, geology, and a single mountain day.
- **Time needed:** two full days; stay longer to compare multiple geopark sectors.
- **Simple plan:** Yinqueshan and central Linyi; then either a Mengshan or Daigu landscape excursion.
- **Main planning issue:** Yimengshan is a large, multi-sector geopark spread across different counties.
- **Editorial view:** one museum plus one carefully selected landscape tells a clearer story than an exhausting circuit of unrelated sites.

## Day one: read the evidence at Yinqueshan

Start with the Yinqueshan Han Tomb Bamboo Slips Museum, subject to its current opening and reservation rules. The Linyi government describes it as a site museum associated with a substantial Han tomb group and displays about the excavated bamboo slips. Read labels closely: original artifacts, replicas, reconstructed tomb settings, and later editions of ancient texts are different kinds of evidence.

The discovery is famous for manuscript versions of military and historical texts, but resist turning every exhibit into a simplified “lost book found” story. Translation and interpretation evolve through conservation and scholarship. Allow time for the archaeological context, writing materials, excavation, and preservation methods, not only celebrated names.

Use the rest of the day for a confirmed-open city museum or public riverfront walk. Check locations carefully because institutional branches and older online addresses may differ.

## Day two: choose one Yimengshan sector

The geopark authority describes Yimengshan as a large property composed of Mengshan, Diamond, Daigu, Menglianggu, and Yunmeng Lake sectors. These are not entrances to one compact park. Choose one according to your interests and the current transport: a maintained Mengshan route for forested granite scenery, or a Daigu route for the region's distinctive flat-topped landforms.

Confirm the exact gate and return trip before leaving Linyi. Within a scenic area, ask which shuttle, cableway, and walking sections operate that day. Avoid unmarked rock edges, disused mines, military-industrial remains, and roads closed for geological hazards. Do not collect minerals or enter research or conservation zones.

## Practical planning

- Check the exact Linyi station and current services on [China Railway 12306](https://www.12306.cn/en/).
- Confirm museum reservations, accepted identification, bag rules, photography, and whether key galleries are open.
- For the geopark, write down the Chinese name of the chosen sector, entrance, pickup point, and last return.
- Carry grippy footwear, water, rain protection, and layers; cableways and entertainment rides are optional, not substitutes for route planning.
- Check the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) for heat, heavy rain, lightning, ice, fog, and geological-risk warnings.
