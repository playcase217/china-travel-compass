Yingkou is best approached as a river-and-sea port city, not simply as a beach stop. Two days can connect the Liaohe Old Street, the late-Qing West Fort, estuary ecology, and one coastal extension without confusing central Yingkou with the separate resort area around Bayuquan.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** port history, modern architecture, estuary landscapes, birdwatching, and a quieter Liaoning coast.
- **Time needed:** two days for the historic city and one selected river or coastal zone.
- **Simple plan:** Liaohe Old Street, waterfront, and West Fort; then wetlands or Bayuquan, not both.
- **Main planning issue:** central Yingkou and Bayuquan are distinct clusters with different stations and substantial transfer time.
- **Editorial view:** the Liaohe estuary and port history are the city's clearest story; beaches are an optional second layer.

## Day one: old port and coastal defense

Start around 营口辽河老街 (Liaohe Old Street), reading surviving and reconstructed streetscapes as evidence of a changing treaty-port and commercial city. Continue along an officially maintained riverside route, staying clear of working docks and restricted port infrastructure.

Visit 西炮台遗址 (West Fort) when the site is confirmed open. Liaoning's cultural authority describes it as a late-Qing coastal-defense work begun in 1882 and completed in 1888. Give the exhibition time to distinguish original fabric from later restoration, and treat the military history as more than a backdrop for photographs.

End near a public estuary viewpoint if conditions allow. Tides, mud, wind, fog, flood controls, and navigation make informal shoreline access unsafe. Never walk onto tidal flats or cross a barrier to approach birds.

## Day two: choose wetlands or the southern coast

For a nature-focused day, use a designated wetland or birdwatching area that is officially open. Keep a long distance from flocks, remain on paths, and avoid drones, playback calls, feeding, or entering reed beds. Migration timing varies with weather and habitat rather than a fixed sightseeing calendar.

For a coastal day, travel toward 鲅鱼圈 (Bayuquan) only after checking the correct station, beach status, water quality notices, tide and weather conditions, and return journey. Shanhai Square and other southern-coast destinations should not be treated as a quick extension of a central-city walk. Swim only where lifeguards and current notices explicitly permit it.

## Practical planning

- Check whether your journey uses 营口东站, 鲅鱼圈站, or another station on [China Railway 12306](https://www.12306.cn/en/).
- Verify West Fort opening, Old Street works, river cruises, special buses, and wetland access through current official notices.
- Carry your passport and allow for security or manual registration.
- Keep away from docks, navigation facilities, mudflats, ice, sea walls during storms, and all fenced industrial land.
- Check the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) plus official tide, wind, flood, and bathing notices.
- Treat cruises, seasonal buses, festivals, and bird concentrations as changeable rather than guaranteed.
