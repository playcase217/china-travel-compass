Zunyi is nationally associated with a decisive 1935 meeting during the Long March, but the city works best when approached as a living regional center rather than a single memorial stop. Two days allow time for careful museum visits, old-city walks, and a broader sense of northern Guizhou's hilly urban landscape.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** modern Chinese history, museums, urban walking, and Guizhou food culture.
- **Time needed:** two days in the city; longer for Maotai, Chishui, or rural conference sites.
- **Simple plan:** conference heritage and nearby streets; then complementary museums and a relaxed city walk.
- **Main planning issue:** high-profile memorial venues may require timed registration and valid identification.
- **Editorial view:** do not force Maotai or Chishui into a short central-Zunyi itinerary.

## Day one: conference heritage in context

Reserve the morning for the 遵义会议纪念馆 (Zunyi Conference Memorial Museum) complex and any confirmed-open associated buildings. Read the introductory chronology before entering the former meeting site; it will make the rooms, documents, and personalities easier to understand.

This is a memorial and educational setting. Follow staff instructions, keep voices low, and obey photography restrictions. Holiday crowd controls, real-name registration, bag checks, and passport handling can change. Confirm the official process shortly before your visit rather than relying on an old screenshot or commercial ticket listing.

Afterward, walk only within the established public old-city area. Notice the steep streets and river-valley topography, and allow extra time in rain. Choose a local restaurant with a clear menu, asking about chili and allergens rather than assuming that every dish can be made mild.

## Day two: broaden the story

Choose one or two confirmed-open public museums or cultural venues that add regional context. A focused visit is more useful than rushing through several memorial halls with overlapping narratives. Look for material on northern Guizhou society, the wider Long March geography, or the development of modern Zunyi.

Use the afternoon for a public riverside, park, or neighborhood walk appropriate to the weather. Zunyi's terrain makes apparently short map distances slower than expected. In heavy rain, replace exposed walking with a cultural venue and avoid informal hillside paths.

Travelers interested in the broader revolutionary landscape can plan a separate day for sites such as Gouba, while spirits enthusiasts can devote an overnight trip to Maotai. Both require current transport and entry research; neither belongs in an improvised half-day detour.

## Practical planning

- Use the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/) and match the exact Zunyi station on your ticket.
- Carry the passport used for reservations; ask the venue how foreign passports are entered if an online form appears ID-card-only.
- Check official venue notices for closure days, timed entry, security screening, and photography rules.
- Wear shoes with grip: old streets and slopes can be slick in rain.
- Verify road conditions before rural memorial excursions, especially during prolonged rain.
- Do not photograph restricted government, transport, or controlled areas.
