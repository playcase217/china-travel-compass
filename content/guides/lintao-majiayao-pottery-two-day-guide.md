Lintao, south of Lanzhou in Dingxi, is a rewarding stop for travelers who want to understand Majiayao painted pottery in the landscape where the archaeological culture was named. The right approach is museum first, protected site only where explicitly open, and no souvenir-shopping claims that confuse modern craft with excavated antiquity.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** painted pottery, archaeology, museum learning, and a slower Gansu route.
- **Time needed:** two days.
- **Simple plan:** Lintao's confirmed-open museum displays; then one authorized Majiayao interpretation site or a Tao River walk.
- **Main planning issue:** similarly named museums, research institutions, villages, and archaeological locations are not interchangeable.
- **Editorial view:** admire modern pottery as contemporary craft and excavated pottery as protected heritage; never blur the two.

## Day one: learn the visual language

Start at 临洮县博物馆 or the current official venue housing its public Majiayao displays, but confirm the address, opening day, reservation method, and passport procedure first. Gansu's culture and tourism department describes sustained archaeological research, museum collections, and exhibitions in its [official account of the Majiayao Culture Festival](https://www.gswbj.gov.cn/a/2023/09/25/18722.html).

Look closely at vessel shape before decoration, then compare line, spiral, net, and geometric arrangements without assigning a fixed symbolic meaning unless the label identifies evidence and interpretation. Dates and cultural phases can be technical; photograph labels only where permitted and preserve their qualifications in your notes.

If you visit a contemporary studio or shop, ask whether pieces are locally made, how they were fired, and whether demonstrations are scheduled. Never buy an item represented as excavated, “ancient,” or removed from a site. Legitimate modern work should be sold as modern work.

## Day two: one authorized heritage stop

Check whether the Majiayao site visitor facilities or another official interpretation venue is open to independent visitors. Public investment documents show that visitor infrastructure has been under development, so an online mention does not prove that a construction or research area is open. Contact the county cultural-tourism authority or venue and follow the entrance they provide.

Archaeological fields, exposed banks, and village land are not open collecting grounds. Remain on signed routes, do not scrape a section or pick up sherds, and do not fly a drone without explicit permission. If access is uncertain, replace the site with more museum time and a public Tao River viewpoint or promenade whose current access is clear.

The [Ministry of Culture and Tourism report on a Lintao painted-pottery exhibition](https://www.mct.gov.cn/wlbphone/wlbydd/xxfb/qglb/hn/202403/t20240319_951768.html) is useful background on the range of prehistoric pottery associated with the county, but a traveling exhibition does not tell you what is on display locally today.

## Transport and route planning

Lintao is usually approached by road, including from Lanzhou; confirm the current legal bus or car option, journey time, drop-off point, and last practical return. Do not publish or rely on an old timetable. Save 临洮县, 临洮县博物馆, 马家窑村, and 洮河 in Chinese.
