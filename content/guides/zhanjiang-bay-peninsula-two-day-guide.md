Zhanjiang is a working port city and the gateway to the Leizhou Peninsula, with a travel identity shaped by the bay, tropical coast, mangroves, layered urban history, and seafood. Two days are enough for the city and one confirmed coastal outing, not a sweep of its many islands.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** maritime cities, coastal ecology, regional food, and slower Guangdong itineraries.
- **Time needed:** two days; add time for islands or the peninsula's far south.
- **Simple plan:** bayfront and city history; one operating mangrove, beach, or island route.
- **Main planning issue:** coastal sites are dispersed and marine transport is weather-dependent.
- **Editorial view:** Zhanjiang makes more sense as a living port and peninsula hub than as a generic beach stop.

## Day one: understand the bay city

Begin with an officially open museum or heritage display, then walk a public section of the bayfront. Zhanjiang's government tourism portal highlights the city's port history, historic schools and streets, craft traditions, maritime folklore, and mangrove identity. Use those themes to read the urban landscape, while distinguishing protected historic buildings from later reconstructions.

Make seafood part of the visit without treating a display tank as proof of sustainability or price. Ask for the price and unit before ordering, confirm whether cooking fees apply, and choose a busy licensed restaurant. If allergies matter, show them in written Chinese and remember that broths and sauces may contain shellfish.

## Day two: choose one coast, wetland, or island

Select a single excursion after checking current conditions. A designated mangrove route offers the clearest ecological focus; a mainland beach reduces ferry risk; an island visit requires confirmed sailings and a dependable final return. The municipal government describes a wider “five islands and one bay” tourism pattern, but that planning concept is not a practical two-day checklist.

Stay on marked boardwalks and public shore access. Mangroves are nurseries and storm buffers: do not enter mudflats, collect wildlife, feed animals, or use a drone where prohibited. Typhoons, lightning, high heat, wind, tides, water quality, and temporary conservation controls can close or alter coastal access.

## Practical planning

- Save 湛江, 湛江西站, 湛江湾, 雷州半岛, and 红树林 in Chinese.
- Confirm trains and the exact Zhanjiang station on the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/).
- Check the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) forecast and official typhoon warnings before any exposed route.
- Confirm ferries directly with the authorized operator on the day of travel; establish the final return before boarding.
- Carry sun protection, drinking water, mosquito repellent, your passport, and a payment backup.

Zhanjiang can follow [Guangzhou](/guides/guangzhou-two-day-itinerary/) or lead into Hainan, but onward ferries and trains should be treated as separate, changeable bookings.
