Qingyang gives travelers a less familiar view of Gansu: deep loess tablelands, eastern Silk Road connections, folk arts, and the North Grotto Temple. A realistic two-day visit pairs the grottoes with city-based context rather than attempting distant sites across the municipality.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** Buddhist sculpture, archaeology, loess geography, museums, paper-cutting, embroidery, and regional history.
- **Time needed:** two days for Qingyang city and the North Grotto Temple.
- **Simple plan:** museums and folk culture in Xifeng; then a prearranged grotto excursion.
- **Main planning issue:** heritage sites across Qingyang are widely dispersed and some are conservation or research locations rather than routine attractions.
- **Editorial view:** make Cave 165 and its historical setting the focus; do not chase a long list of remote sites.

## Day one: understand eastern Gansu

Start at a confirmed-open municipal museum, cultural center, or official exhibition in 西峰区 (Xifeng District). Look for context on the loess landscape, early settlement, Silk Road-era exchange, and Qingyang's living traditions. The city is associated with paper-cutting, sachet embroidery, shadow puppetry, and other folk arts, but buy from accountable makers and ask before photographing artisans or performances.

Use the afternoon for an ordinary city walk and trip preparation. Confirm the next day's vehicle, the precise visitor entrance, opening status, photography rules, and return plan with the responsible institution. A map pin alone is not sufficient for a protected grotto site.

## Day two: North Grotto Temple

北石窟寺 (North Grotto Temple) lies outside central Qingyang. The city's current visitor guide describes a complex of grottoes and niches carved into sandstone cliffs, with Cave 165 as its monumental early center. Give the site a half or full day, depending on current access and interpretation.

Conservation comes first. Do not touch stonework, cross barriers, use flash, or assume every cave is open. Heavy rain, freeze-thaw damage, maintenance, or archaeological work can restrict the route. The valleys and loess slopes around the site are part of its setting, but they are not permission to enter closed land or scramble above the caves.

## Practical planning

- Use [China Railway 12306](https://www.12306.cn/en/) to verify current trains and the exact Qingyang station.
- Arrange a licensed vehicle or confirmed local transport for the grottoes; agree on the return before departure.
- Carry the passport used for bookings and ask whether advance registration is required.
- Follow the site's current rules on bags, photography, tripods, drones, and accessible caves.
- Check the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) for heat, storms, snow, and road conditions.
- Treat other sites such as Nanzuo, county museums, or revolutionary-history destinations as separate trips unless their access and travel time are confirmed.
