Yangjiang offers more than a beach break. Hailing Island brings together the Guangdong Maritime Silk Road Museum, the Nanhai No. 1 shipwreck story, fishing-community culture, mangroves, and exposed South China Sea beaches. The best two-day plan gives maritime archaeology its own half-day and treats sea safety as a decision, not a footnote.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** maritime history, museums, coastal landscapes, seafood, and a Guangdong side trip.
- **Time needed:** two days; add a night if your rail arrival is late.
- **Simple plan:** Yangjiang orientation; Hailing Island museum and one managed coastal area.
- **Main planning issue:** typhoons, wind, heat, currents, and transfers between the station, city, and island.
- **Editorial view:** the museum is the anchor; beach time depends on conditions.

## Day one: arrive and understand Yangjiang

Use your arrival day for central Yangjiang. Choose a confirmed-open museum, cultural venue, or established public street circuit, then explore the city's food and craft traditions without turning commercial labels into historical facts. The municipal government highlights local knife making, kites, fermented soybean products, and coastal foodways, but individual workshops and exhibitions require current confirmation.

Before dinner, settle the next day's transport to 海陵岛 (Hailing Island). Save your exact museum or hotel entrance in Chinese. “Hailing Island” is too broad a destination for a driver.

## Day two: shipwreck archaeology before the beach

Start with 广东海上丝绸之路博物馆 (Guangdong Maritime Silk Road Museum), built around the excavation, conservation, and interpretation of the Song-period Nanhai No. 1 shipwreck. Allow real museum time; it provides the reason this coast belongs in a cultural itinerary.

Then select one officially managed coastal area. 大角湾 (Dajiao Bay) is a developed option, while mangrove or fishing-port visits offer a different focus. Do not attempt to combine every island attraction. Swim only where lifeguards and authorities permit it, obey flags and barriers, and stay out of the water during warnings.

The coast is exposed to tropical storms and intense sun. A clear morning does not guarantee a safe afternoon. If the museum or beach closes, accept the closure and use an inland cultural alternative.

## Practical planning

- Check your train and station through the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/).
- Confirm museum reservations, passport acceptance, and opening days through its current official channel.
- Ask for a metered taxi or book through a recognized platform; verify the return from the island in advance.
- Photograph seafood prices and confirm whether they are per item, portion, or weight before ordering.
- Check the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/), local typhoon notices, beach flags, and marine warnings.
- Never enter closed water, cliffs, reefs, mangrove zones, or working harbor areas.
