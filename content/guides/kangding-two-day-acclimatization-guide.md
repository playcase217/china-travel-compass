Kangding works best as a place to slow down, not merely a transfer point on the way to western Sichuan's higher grasslands. Give the city two nights, keep the first day gentle, and decide on any mountain excursion only after seeing how your body responds to the elevation.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** a first highland stop, mountain-city walks, Tibetan and Han cultural context, and onward-trip preparation.
- **Time needed:** two nights and one or two low-intensity days.
- **Simple plan:** central Kangding and its religious sites first; one managed nearby landscape only if conditions are good.
- **Main planning issue:** altitude, rapidly changing mountain weather, and long road transfers matter more than distance on a map.
- **Editorial view:** an unhurried city day is useful acclimatization, not a day wasted before the “real” trip.

## Day one: stay close to the city

After arrival, check in, eat lightly, hydrate normally, and walk only at a conversational pace. Explore signed public streets around the river and old commercial center, then choose one or two religious or cultural sites from the municipal government's city route, such as Anjue Temple, Jingang Temple, or Nanwu Temple. Confirm current public access locally.

These are active religious places rather than stage sets. Dress modestly, keep voices low, follow the direction used by worshippers where indicated, and ask before photographing people, ceremonies, or interiors. Do not touch offerings or climb onto religious structures for a photograph.

Avoid alcohol, an intense workout, and a tightly timed evening. Headache, nausea, unusual fatigue, dizziness, or poor sleep can be altitude-related. Stop ascending and seek medical advice if symptoms concern you; severe breathlessness at rest, confusion, loss of coordination, or worsening symptoms require urgent medical attention and descent.

## Day two: choose one modest outing

If you feel well, choose one officially open, managed attraction or a second city walk. Paoma Mountain may look close to town, but confirm the entrance, operating transport, trail condition, and weather before setting out. Mugecuo and places farther toward Tagong or Xinduqiao are not casual add-ons: road time, elevation gain, traffic, and weather can turn them into full-day commitments.

Do not combine several high viewpoints simply because a driver proposes a loop. Private vehicles should be licensed and arranged through a reputable operator or accommodation. Confirm the exact Chinese destination, return time, inclusions, and whether the route enters areas affected by snow, ice, roadworks, fire controls, or temporary access rules.

## Getting there and moving onward

Verify rail options, station names, passport ticketing, and current services through the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/). Road services from Chengdu and elsewhere can change with construction and mountain conditions; confirm them through your accommodation or an official local transport channel shortly before travel.

Kangding is the Ganzi prefectural seat and a transport gateway, but onward places are spread across large terrain. Build a buffer before flights or other fixed bookings. Save 康定市, 跑马山, and your hotel address in Chinese for drivers.

## Weather and responsible travel

Check forecasts and warnings through the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) and ask locally about road conditions. Sun, rain, hail, snow, and strong wind can occur in quick succession. Carry layers, rain protection, sun protection, water, and offline maps.

Stay on open roads and marked trails. Do not enter pasture, camps, villages, or mountain tracks without permission; do not fly a drone near airports, religious sites, protected areas, settlements, or security-sensitive locations without explicit authorization.
