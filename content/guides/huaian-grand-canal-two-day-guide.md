Huai'an makes the Grand Canal legible. Rather than offering one famous postcard, it connects working waterways, the history of grain transport, an old administrative city, and the broad landscapes around Hongze Lake. Two days are enough for a coherent introduction if you keep the historic Huai'an District and the modern city-center waterfront as separate clusters.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** canal history, museums, old streets, water engineering, and travelers crossing northern Jiangsu.
- **Time needed:** two days for the city; add a third if Hongze Lake is a priority.
- **Simple plan:** the canal waterfront and a museum on day one; Huai'an District and Hexia Old Town on day two.
- **Main planning issue:** attractions labeled “Huai'an” can be well outside the current urban center.
- **Editorial view:** come to understand a transport system, not to collect every reconstructed waterfront sight.

## Day one: follow the living canal

Begin along an officially open section of the Li Canal cultural corridor. Look for locks, embankments, bridges, retained port structures, and modern shipping before entering a museum. Huai'an's value lies partly in seeing heritage and contemporary water management side by side.

Choose one substantial canal or city-history museum for the afternoon. Exhibition names, entry procedures, and English interpretation can change, so check the institution's official account or ask your accommodation to call. A translation app is useful even when object labels are bilingual.

Stay near the central waterfront for an evening walk. Treat sightseeing cruises as optional: routes depend on water conditions, maintenance, and seasonal operations, and they do not replace seeing the canal from land.

## Day two: Huai'an District and Hexia

Travel to the historic Huai'an District and group **Hexia Old Town**, the former government-office area, and a canal-transport museum only if their current openings form a sensible walking or short-taxi route. Hexia rewards attention to lane patterns, courtyards, food traditions, and the relationship between merchants and the canal more than a rushed photo circuit.

Several memorial halls and former residences in the district cover nationally important historical or literary figures. Select one according to your interests, approach it as a place of interpretation rather than a generic attraction, and carry your passport. Do not assume every venue accepts the same reservation method.

Hongze Lake Ancient Weir is a separate excursion, not an effortless add-on. Choose it instead of the old-city cluster if hydraulic history and open water are your main interests; confirm the exact entrance and return transport before departing.

## Practical planning

- Search 淮安 for Huai'an, 里运河 for the Li Canal, 河下古镇 for Hexia Old Town, and 洪泽湖古堰 for Hongze Lake Ancient Weir.
- Pin each destination in Chinese and compare travel times before choosing a hotel.
- Confirm your exact station and train through the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/).
- Carry your passport, rain protection, drinking water, and a payment backup.
- Check the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) forecast and official flood or navigation notices after heavy rain.

Huai'an can complement [Yangzhou](/guides/yangzhou-two-day-itinerary/) or [Nanjing](/guides/nanjing-two-day-itinerary/), but it deserves its own overnight stay.
