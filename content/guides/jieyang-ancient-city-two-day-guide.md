Jieyang offers a grounded introduction to Chaoshan culture without requiring a checklist of famous landmarks. Its old-city core brings together an academy, temples, arcaded streets, markets, food, and overseas-Chinese memory. Two days leave room for early mornings, long meals, and the small architectural details that disappear on a hurried circuit.

## At a glance

- **Best for:** walkable heritage, living religious spaces, Chaoshan food, craft, and street photography.
- **Time needed:** two days centered on Rongcheng.
- **Simple plan:** the ancient-city core first; a museum, market, and one nearby neighborhood second.
- **Main planning issue:** restoration and event work can alter pedestrian routes and access to individual interiors.
- **Editorial view:** Jieyang works best as a lived-in city, not a substitute for a theme park.

## Day one: walk the ancient city slowly

Begin around **Jinxian Gate** and continue on foot toward **Jieyang Academy**, arcaded streets, and officially open heritage buildings. The Guangdong foreign-affairs authority describes the core as a concentration of protected sites and layers from the Song period through the Republic era. On-site labels are the best guide to what survives, what has been reconstructed, and what has recently been restored.

Temples remain active community spaces. Dress respectfully, keep clear of worship, and ask before photographing people, rituals, or interiors. Do not assume every old doorway is a public attraction. Finish with a Chaoshan dinner and gongfu tea at a pace that lets the meal be part of the cultural experience.

## Day two: objects, food, and ordinary streets

Choose a municipal museum, cultural center, or officially announced exhibition to add context. Then visit a morning market or established food street, keeping bags close and avoiding disruption to vendors. Useful dishes to explore can include rice-noodle rolls, oyster omelet, beef products, braised dishes, and local sweets, but availability varies by shop and time.

Travelers with dietary restrictions should show written Chinese requirements and ask specifically about broth, lard, shellfish, peanuts, and cross-contact. “No meat” does not automatically mean a dish is vegetarian.

Spend the afternoon on one neighborhood, riverside walk, or craft stop rather than making an ambitious trip to Puning, Huilai, or Jiexi. The wider municipality is large and deserves separate transport planning.

## Practical planning

- Search 揭阳 for Jieyang, 揭阳古城 for the old city, 进贤门 for Jinxian Gate, and 揭阳学宫 for Jieyang Academy.
- Confirm your railway station carefully; names containing Jieyang may serve different parts of the municipality.
- Use the [official China Railway 12306 English site](https://www.12306.cn/en/) for present trains and identity-document rules.
- Carry rain protection, drinking water, sun protection, and a payment backup.
- Check the [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.cma.gov.cn/en/) forecast and official typhoon or extreme-rain notices.

Jieyang can form a slower regional sequence with [Chaozhou](/guides/chaozhou-two-day-old-city-guide/) and [Shantou](/guides/shantou-two-day-chaoshan-guide/).
