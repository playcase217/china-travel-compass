English-speaking visitors can travel around China much more smoothly with a small set of apps prepared before arrival. Start with Alipay and WeChat for payments and everyday services, add DiDi for ride-hailing, install a translation tool, and treat Chinese-first apps such as Dianping and Meituan as useful optional layers rather than essential starting points.

## At a glance

Install Alipay, WeChat, DiDi, a translation app, and a map option before your flight. Link an eligible international card to both Alipay and WeChat Pay where supported, but keep a physical bank card and some cash as backups. Use DiDi for taxis and ride-hailing. Use Dianping for restaurant discovery and Meituan for local services only when you are comfortable navigating more Chinese-language screens.

## The practical app stack

You do not need every popular Chinese app on day one. A reliable visitor setup has three levels:

- **Install before departure:** Alipay, WeChat, DiDi, translation, and maps.
- **Use after arrival if helpful:** Dianping and Meituan.
- **Keep as backups:** physical cards, modest cash, hotel addresses saved in Chinese, and offline screenshots.

App interfaces, card support, and identity-verification steps can change. Complete setup early enough that you can solve any issue before a busy sightseeing day.

## Alipay: make this your first payment app

Alipay is a strong first choice for visitors because its international-facing guidance is clear and it can serve as a practical gateway to payments and selected travel services.

Set up your account, use your passport details if identity verification is requested, and add an eligible international bank card. After setup, you can use QR payments at many shops, restaurants, attractions, and everyday businesses.

Keep in mind that not every feature available to a long-term resident will necessarily work the same way for a visitor. Start with payments, then explore additional services only when you need them.

## WeChat and WeChat Pay: communication plus payments

WeChat is widely used for messaging, business communication, QR-based interactions, and mini programs. It is worth installing even if you already use Alipay for most payments.

Where supported, visitors can add eligible international cards to WeChat Pay. Keep WeChat as a second payment route rather than depending on only one wallet. It can also help when a hotel, guide, or local contact prefers to communicate through WeChat.

Some mini programs are mainly designed for Chinese-language users. Use translation tools and avoid assuming every mini program will accept a foreign phone number or international card.

## DiDi: the easiest ride-hailing option

DiDi is the most useful ride-hailing app for many visitors. It reduces the need to explain destinations verbally and creates a digital trip record.

Use the standalone DiDi app or an available in-app entry point inside a larger platform such as Alipay. Before requesting a ride:

- Confirm the pickup point carefully, especially at airports, railway stations, and large malls.
- Save your hotel name and address in Chinese.
- Check the vehicle plate number before entering.
- Allow extra time when road traffic is heavy.

For Beijing arrival logistics, use the [airport-to-city guide](/guides/beijing-airport-to-city/).

## Dianping: discover restaurants like a local

Dianping, commonly written as `大众点评`, is valuable for restaurant discovery, reviews, photos, opening times, and nearby searches. It can reveal useful options beyond the restaurants that appear in English-language travel guides.

The tradeoff is language. Dianping is primarily designed for Chinese users. English speakers can still use it effectively with screen translation, photo browsing, and copied place names.

A practical workflow:

1. Search using the Chinese name of a dish, neighborhood, or restaurant.
2. Compare recent photos and review volume.
3. Copy the address into your map or ride-hailing app.
4. Confirm opening times before traveling across the city.

For a first Beijing food experience, start with the [roast duck guide](/guides/beijing-roast-duck-guide/).

## Meituan: useful, but optional for short trips

Meituan, commonly written as `美团`, is a major local-services platform. It can cover food delivery, restaurant offers, attraction-related listings, and other everyday services.

For a short trip, it is optional. The interface and service flows may be easier for travelers who read Chinese, have a local phone number, or are staying long enough to justify extra setup.

Use it when it solves a real problem, such as finding nearby food options or exploring delivery choices at a hotel. Do not make your first day depend on it.

## Translation: install a tool with offline support

A translation app is not only for conversations. It also helps with menus, signs, Chinese-first app screens, and addresses.

Before arrival:

- Download an offline Chinese language pack if your tool supports it.
- Practice camera translation.
- Save your hotel's Chinese name and address.
- Keep screenshots of the most important destinations.

## Maps: choose a workable combination

Map availability and feature quality can vary depending on your phone and region. Test your preferred map app before the trip, but save important addresses in Chinese as a backup.

For complex journeys, compare the map route with official transport information. In Beijing, the [subway guide](/guides/beijing-subway-guide/) explains the practical habits that make city travel easier.

## Avoid common setup mistakes

- Do not rely on only one payment app.
- Do not wait until you are standing at a restaurant counter to add a bank card.
- Do not assume every Chinese app has a complete English interface.
- Do not share verification codes, passwords, or payment PINs with anyone.
- Do not install unofficial copies of apps from unknown websites.
- Do not depend on food delivery immediately after landing.

## A simple pre-flight checklist

- Install Alipay and add an eligible international card.
- Install WeChat and set up WeChat Pay where supported.
- Install DiDi and confirm you can access the ride-booking flow.
- Install translation with offline Chinese support.
- Test your preferred map option.
- Save your first hotel's Chinese address and phone number.
- Bring a backup physical card and modest cash.

With this setup, the major parts of independent travel become much easier: paying for meals, reaching your hotel, navigating cities, and finding useful local information without needing perfect Chinese.
